How to Record Yourself Sleeping on iPhone: 3 Ways Compared

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 14 Nov 2026

Quick answer

Yes. An iPhone can record you sleeping three ways: Voice Memos, a Camera video, or a sleep recorder app that labels what it hears. For a whole night, use a sleep recorder. Apple doesn’t say how long Voice Memos or video can record or how much battery a night takes, and neither one marks the moments worth hearing. I make Snore Timeline, which keeps recording with the screen locked, labels snoring, sleep talking and gasps on a timeline, and uses about 3 to 5% of your battery an hour.

Key takeaways

  • Three ways: Voice Memos and the Camera come with the iPhone. A sleep recorder app also labels the sounds, so you can jump straight to them.
  • Voice Memos: recording stops if another app starts playing audio [1]. Skip Silence skips the quiet parts when you play it back [2].
  • Video: you can’t record video during a phone or FaceTime call [4]. For a dark room, the only setting Apple offers for regular video is a lower frame rate [5].
  • Snore Timeline: a night takes about 64 to 200 MB, depending on the quality setting, and you can play back every minute.
  • Privacy: a microphone records everyone in the room. Ask a partner before you record.

    How to record yourself sleeping with Snore Timeline

    Set it up once, then it’s one tap a night. The steps follow the Getting Started guide.

    1. Install it and allow the microphone. Until you allow it, the record button stays grayed out. You need iOS 18 or later and about 500 MB free.
    2. Plug the phone in. When power runs low, iOS may close background apps and end the recording. Adaptive Power can also limit background activity and turn on Low Power Mode at 20% [9].
    3. Turn on Sleep Focus. Calls and alerts then can’t grab the microphone in the night.
    4. Pick a quality tier. Basic (about 8 MB an hour) keeps the snoring range. Pick High Fidelity (about 25 MB an hour) if you want to hear your breathing.
    5. Tap Record and lock the phone. The green button turns into a red stop button, and recording carries on with the screen off.
    6. Set it face down, 1 to 2 feet from your head. Face down turns off the Always-On display [8], which roughly halves the drain. For snoring alone, 3 to 4 feet works.
    7. In the morning, tap stop. The app builds the night’s summary and timeline.

    Two settings save a step. Recording Delay holds off the start for up to 180 minutes, so the time you spend reading stays out. A Shortcuts automation can run Start Recording at the same time every night (Shortcuts setup). If you’re recording to check for snoring and want to bring it to a doctor, the snoring recording guide covers placement and export.

    How to record yourself sleeping with Voice Memos

    Voice Memos needs no download. Open it, tap the record button and put the phone down [1].

    • Close your audio apps: if another app starts playing audio, Voice Memos stops recording [1]. A sleep podcast on a timer will end the night’s file.
    • Test it on a nap: Apple’s guide doesn’t say how long a memo can run or whether it keeps going with the screen locked.
    • Switch to Mono for review: Skip Silence and Enhance Recording work on Mono and Stereo recordings [2]. Newer iPhones record Spatial Audio by default; change it in Settings, Apps, Voice Memos, Recording Mode [1].
    • Plan the morning: you get one long file with no labels. Skip Silence and a faster playback speed shorten the search [2].

    How to video record yourself sleeping

    The Camera works if you need to see movement. Prop the phone on a stable surface, plugged in, with your bed in frame, and record in Video or Time-lapse mode [4].

    • Leave some light: Auto FPS drops video to 24 fps in low light [5], and on supported models Night mode works for time-lapse [6]. Apple names no mode for a dark room.
    • Silence calls: video recording isn’t available during a phone or FaceTime call [4].
    • Watch the heat: Apple lists long camera use among the causes of a hot iPhone. When it overheats, charging can slow or stop [7].
    • Keep the default: video records at 30 fps by default, and higher frame rates and resolutions make larger files [4] [5].

    What you’ll find in the morning

    Snore Timeline lays the night out as a waveform you scroll and tap to play (timeline and playback).

    • Snoring episodes: each with its length, snore count and loudness, ready to play.
    • Sleep talking, coughs, gasps and laughter: each gets its own label. Turn on Loud Sound Detection, off by default, to mark loud noises the app can’t name.
    • Breathing disruptions: 8 seconds or more that sound like a blocked airway, silent or not, found by a model trained on sleep-lab recordings. An audio pattern to show a doctor, never a diagnosis (how flags work).
    • The unexpected: pets, partners and the heating show up too. The guide to noises you hear on a sleep recording helps you work out what each one was.

    Keep the recording private

    Snore Timeline declares Data Not Collected on the App Store [10]. It has no account and no server, so recordings stay on your phone until you export them. Voice Memos saves recordings to iCloud when Voice Memos is on in iCloud settings [1], and its label lists Audio Data as Data Linked to You [3].

    Ask a partner before you record. The microphone hears everyone in the room, and a camera sees them.

    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. Bring recordings to a doctor if you suspect breathing pauses.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does a sleep recorder drain the battery?

    It uses some, and the comparison table has Snore Timeline’s figure. Plug in either way, since Apple publishes no overnight figure for Voice Memos or video.

    Can I record myself sleeping with the iPhone locked?

    Snore Timeline records with the screen locked, whether or not the phone is plugged in. Apple doesn’t say either way for Voice Memos, so test it on a nap first.

    What’s the best free sleep recorder app?

    Look for one that keeps the whole night and plays it back without paying; Snore Timeline does both. SnoreLab’s free version limits how many recordings you can play each night, and after five sessions it can’t run two nights in a row [11].

    How do I video record myself sleeping?

    Use the Camera’s Video or Time-lapse mode with the phone plugged in and some light in the room. The video steps cover calls and heat.
