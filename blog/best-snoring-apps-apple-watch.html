Best Snoring Apps for Apple Watch Wearers (2026)

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 9 Oct 2026

Quick answer

Apple Watch doesn’t record snoring, and no app can make it. You need an iPhone app to record the sound while the watch adds heart rate, blood oxygen, sleep stages and wrist taps. Snore Timeline, which I built, does this with no subscription or in-app purchases and lines the watch readings up next to the audio. Sleep Cycle merges the two as well if you have iOS 18 and Premium. The other four apps here each miss a piece, from a paywall on the recorder to no recorder at all. I based this on 1,162 App Store reviews from Watch owners.

Key takeaways

  • Microphone: no Apple Watch sleep feature uses the microphone. The FDA summary lists the accelerometer as the only input to Apple’s sleep apnea notification [3].
  • Reviews: I read 1,162 App Store reviews that mention the watch, apnea, oxygen or heart rate. The top wish was snoring sound from the phone and wrist data from the watch for the same night.
  • Same night: Snore Timeline and Sleep Cycle are the only two of the six apps that record snoring on the iPhone and use the watch readings from the same night.
  • Apple’s notification: in Apple’s study it flagged 66% of people with sleep apnea, including 89% of severe cases and 43% of moderate ones. It cleared 98.5% of people without sleep apnea.
  • Deep sleep: it’s the stage reviewers doubt most. Two independent lab studies found the watch caught about 50% of it.
  • Charging: seven reviewers lost a night because the watch died or sat on the charger. Charge it to at least 30% before bed. Apple records no sleep data until you’ve slept an hour with the watch on.

Reddit blocks automated reading, so it isn’t in the sample.

    How accurate is the watch on deep sleep?

    Deep sleep is the stage reviewers doubt most. On Apple Community (2 Jun 2023), owners of the Series 7, Series 8 and Ultra reported near-zero deep sleep from Apple. AutoSleep, Fitbit or Garmin gave them 45 minutes to two hours. One concluded that Apple files too much of his deep sleep under Core, Apple’s name for light sleep [23]. A Sleep++ reviewer (6 Jul 2025) ran five apps off one watch and got five different answers.

    What does Apple’s sleep apnea notification catch, and what does it miss?

    Apple rates each night as Elevated or Not Elevated under Breathing Disturbances in the Health app. It sends one notification if a 30-day window has enough Elevated nights. The windows are fixed blocks of 30 days, not a rolling count, and the first one starts 30 days after you turn the feature on. No microphone is involved.

    What forum users ran into

    • Numbers with no explanation. One user’s first two nights were both Elevated, at 48.78 and 39.33, and the user couldn’t find what the values meant. A reply listed five Not Elevated nights between 0.59 and 2.28 [22].
    • A quiet month read as a clean bill of health. An Ultra 2 wearer saw no Elevated night in six weeks and concluded he was clear. The feature can’t promise that [21].
    • The hand-off Apple intended. One user who got the notification sent Apple’s exported PDF to his doctor that morning [21].

    Only one of the 1,162 reviews mentions Breathing Disturbances. For now, people discuss the feature on forums. Apnea indicators in other watch-only apps can miss cases too. A US NapBot review from 21 Aug 2023 says its indicator missed moderate sleep apnea that a sleep study later confirmed.     The six apps, with the watch on

    For each app: what it does on the watch, what sits behind the paywall, its privacy label and what its reviewers in my sample said.

    Snore Timeline

    Snore Timeline records on the iPhone or through AirPods. It marks snores, gasps, coughs, sleep talking and breathing disruptions on a timeline you can play back. After a 15-minute baseline it estimates sleep stages from your breathing sounds, with or without the watch.

    • Free tier: everything, including full-night audio, CSV and clip export to Files, Snore Nudge and the Smart Wake Up Alarm.
    • Price: none. No subscription or in-app purchases.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected, as declared on the App Store.
    • Reviews: I left my own app’s reviews out of the sample.
    • Watch: a recovery score built from HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, wrist temperature and time asleep, each against your own baseline. Those vitals sit beside the snoring timeline. Watch stages stay separate from the audio stages, so you can compare the two. Snore Nudge taps your wrist when snoring keeps going.

    Sleep Cycle

    Sleep Cycle is known for its smart alarm. On the watch it tracks sleep with the accelerometer [9].

    • Free tier: smart alarm, basic analysis and a nightly graph [11].
    • Price: Premium, $2.99 to $57.99. Premium adds snore detection, Who’s Snoring (which separates a partner’s snores), heart rate, respiratory rate and automatic watch tracking.
    • Privacy label: Data Used to Track You: Identifiers. Data Linked to You: Health & Fitness, Purchases, Location, Contact Info, Identifiers, Usage Data, Diagnostics.
    • Reviews: 42 of the 1,162 reviews mention the price. Reviewers praise the alarm’s long track record.
    • Watch: wrist motion times the smart alarm and draws the nightly sleep graph for free. Heart rate tracking needs Premium [11]. Snore Timeline also times its alarm to light sleep. Nights tracked by the watch alone have no snoring, ambient noise or breathing data, because only the iPhone records those [9]. Phone and watch sessions merge only on iOS 18 and watchOS 11, and only if you start the watch session first [10].

    Pillow

    Pillow can track a night from the watch alone, and it has an Editors’ Choice badge. It can’t record audio in automatic mode, so each night you choose: the watch tracks automatically, or you use manual mode and the iPhone microphone records [17].

    • Free tier: sleep tracking. A UK reviewer (25 Jun 2025) found the free version does no more than the watch already does.
    • Price: Premium, $19.99 to $59.99. Premium adds audio recordings, heart rate analysis and a smart alarm [12].
    • Privacy label: Data Linked to You: Identifiers, Usage Data, Diagnostics. Data Not Linked to You: Purchases, Location, User Content, Identifiers.
    • Reviews: 93 of the 1,162 reviews complain about the subscription, the most of any app here.
    • Watch: Premium heart rate analysis beside your sleep stages, with last night’s report on the wrist [17]. Snore Timeline shows overnight heart rate against your own baseline for free.

    SnoreLab

    SnoreLab has no Watch app [18], but it calibrated its Breathing Disturbance Index on Apple Watch data [13].

    • Free tier: records a night on the iPhone, shows charts and plays audio. After the first five sessions it can’t record two nights in a row [13].
    • Price: Premium, $5.99 to $59.99, or a one-time purchase. Premium adds unlimited recording and full history.
    • Privacy label: Data Used to Track You: Usage Data. Data Linked to You: Health & Fitness, Contact Info, User Content, Identifiers.
    • Reviews: 110 of the 186 reviews that mention apnea are SnoreLab’s. Many pair watch oxygen and heart rate with their phone recordings [18]. One reviewer (29 Sep 2026) wishes the watch would buzz at loud snoring.
    • Watch: Health data only. The watch’s sleep phases appear on the snoring chart, as watch stages do in Snore Timeline. It has no wrist taps, live view or wrist alarm [13].

    SleepWatch

    SleepWatch is built around the watch. It reads heart rate dip, HRV, blood oxygen and sleep disruption from the wrist, and its iPhone app records snoring [14].

    • Free tier: watch sleep tracking. Snore Tracking and the Smart Alarm for Apple Watch need Premium [14].
    • Price: Premium, $3.99 to $39.99.
    • Privacy label: Data Linked to You: Health & Fitness, User Content. Not Linked: Usage Data.
    • Reviews: 90 of the 1,162 reviews complain about the subscription. SleepWatch accounts for 47 of the 104 missed nights, mostly after updates [19]. One reviewer (30 Jul 2026) was billed $40 after using it once.
    • Watch: it tracks how far your heart rate dips in sleep and how regular your sleep times are [19]. The SleepWatch Score needs Premium [14]. Snore Timeline’s free sleep score also weighs bedtime regularity. No help article says whether the iPhone recorder and the watch tracker can run on the same night.

    AutoSleep

    AutoSleep tracks stages, heart rate and readiness from the watch with nothing to press, and it has a smart alarm [15]. It records no sound. For snoring you need its $4.99 sister app, AutoSnore, so sleep and snoring sit in two separate apps.

    • Free tier: none. It’s a paid app.
    • Price: One-time, $8.99, no in-app purchases [20].
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected.
    • Reviews: 59 of the 1,162 reviews mention price, most of them praising the single payment. Two reviewers found its total sleep within minutes of a clinic sleep study [20]. Another (24 Jun 2026) saw oxygen dips that a sleep study confirmed.
    • Watch: the watch is the whole app. It rates each night from time asleep, deep sleep and heart rate [20], keeps a sleep bank, and gives a readiness score from HRV and waking pulse against your baseline [26]. Snore Timeline has a sleep score, a sleep bank and a recovery score too. AutoSnore has no Watch app.

    Which one to pick

    • Pick Snore Timeline if you want snoring audio from the phone and the watch’s vitals and stages in one morning summary, with no subscription. You also get a wrist tap when you snore, and sleep stages from the audio on nights you leave the watch off. Oura, Garmin, Whoop and Ultrahuman readings arrive through Apple Health the same way.
    • Pick Sleep Cycle if you want a Philips Hue light or HomeKit scene to wake you and will pay for Premium, which also unlocks snore detection and the merged watch session.
    • Pick Pillow if you want the watch to track sleep automatically without the iPhone, and you can live without audio on those nights.
    • Pick SnoreLab if you want a dedicated snore app with a long track record and 57K ratings.
    • Pick AutoSleep if you want the watch alone to track sleep, with no phone by the bed, and you don’t need sound.

    Set it up so no night goes missing

    These workarounds come from the reviews and forums, plus Apple’s published rules.

    1. Charge before bed. Apple asks you to charge the watch to at least 30% before bed. It records sleep only after you’ve slept at least an hour with the watch on [4]. Reviewers charge during the evening wind-down or the first hour of the morning. One MacRumors user (1 Nov 2024) alternates an Ultra 2 and an old Series 7 so a watch is always on the wrist at night [21].
    2. Let the phone hear and the watch feel. Put the phone 1 to 2 feet from your side of the bed to catch breathing sounds, or 3 to 4 feet if you only want snoring. Wear the watch for vitals. Snore Timeline needs no extra setup for this. Sleep Cycle needs the watch session started first [10], and Pillow needs manual mode to record any audio [17].
    3. Start the recording on the phone. iOS needs the phone app open to start a recording, so Snore Timeline’s Watch app can stop a recording but can’t start one. Overnight the Watch app shows live heart rate and HRV. In the morning it shows a dashboard.
    4. Don’t expect the stages to match. Apple finalizes its sleep stages one to three hours after you wake, so a 6 a.m. summary may not include them yet. Two sources will also disagree on deep sleep, and the lab checks explain why.
    5. Prove whose snoring it is. Play the clip back. A phone 1 to 2 feet from you hears you far louder than a partner a yard farther away, so move it to your side or record with AirPods. One SnoreLab reviewer (27 Dec 2023) sleeps in a separate room to get a clean score [18]. Sleep Cycle’s advice for a loud partner is to switch to accelerometer tracking, which gives up the sound [25]. One couple swapped watches for a night (17 Feb 2024) to prove the data was theirs [20].
    6. Give the apnea notification a full month. Turn on Track Sleep and wear the watch at least ten nights. Check Health > Browse > Respiratory for Breathing Disturbances instead of waiting for a notification [1]. If you get one, export the PDF and book a home sleep test. If a partner hears you gasp, book one even without a notification [24].
    7. Already diagnosed? Read your CPAP data instead. Apple’s notification is for adults without a diagnosis [1]. CPAP users on the forums read their machine’s own data [21]. SnoreLab reviewers on CPAP use the recordings to catch nights the mask slipped [18], and one in Australia (6 Dec 2022) found the machine itself logged as snoring all night. Snore Timeline’s sleep factors compare nights you tag CPAP with untagged nights once you’ve recorded five.
    Tip

    Open the Watch app on your iPhone and set Sounds & Haptics to Prominent. Every wrist tap gets firmer. That includes Snore Nudge, which lets you shift position without a sound that wakes your partner, and the Smart Wake Up Alarm, which can wake you with taps alone within a 15, 30 or 45 minute window. That one setting is most of the difference between a tap you sleep through and one you feel.

    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. Apple says the same of its sleep apnea notification. Bring recordings, and the Health app’s exported PDF, to a doctor if you suspect breathing pauses.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can Apple Watch detect snoring on its own?

    No. None of Apple’s sleep features use the microphone, and a third-party Watch app can’t record audio in the background either. An iPhone app records the snoring, and the watch adds the rest.

    My watch says Elevated breathing disturbances. Do I have sleep apnea?

    Not from that alone. Alcohol, medications and sleep position can push a night to Elevated. The notification itself waits for a 30-day window with ten or more nights worn, at least half of them Elevated. A quiet month doesn’t clear you either. If you get the notification, or a partner hears you gasp, ask a doctor about a home sleep test. The notification numbers show what it misses.

    Do I need an Apple Watch to use Snore Timeline?

    No. It records through the iPhone or AirPods and estimates sleep stages from breathing sounds. A watch adds vitals and wrist taps, and other wearables feed in through Apple Health.

    Which apps combine phone snoring audio with Apple Watch data on the same night?

    Snore Timeline does it on every night a watch is paired, with no setting to change. Sleep Cycle does it with Premium on iOS 18 and watchOS 11 if you start the watch session first. In both, the iPhone records the sound and the watch adds its readings. The app notes cover the other four.

    Why does my deep sleep differ between the watch and a sleep app?

    Every source estimates deep sleep, and deep sleep is where the estimates are weakest. Apps that read the watch also add their own rules on top. The lab checks give the numbers. Snore Timeline keeps each source’s stage timeline separate so you can compare them.

    The watch died overnight. Is the night lost?

    For watch-only trackers, yes. In Snore Timeline the recording runs on the phone, so the snoring, the audio-based stages and the timeline are there in the morning. Only the wrist readings are missing.
