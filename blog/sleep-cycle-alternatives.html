Sleep Cycle Alternatives for Snore Tracking: 8 Apps Compared

Sleep Cycle pairs a smart alarm with snore detection. These eight apps cover one or both, and I list what each charges and declares.

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 28 Sep 2026

Quick answer

If you're leaving Sleep Cycle but still want snore tracking, the options are Snore Timeline, Pillow, ShutEye, BetterSleep, SnoreLab, Snollo, AutoSleep and, on Android, Sleep as Android. Snore Timeline records the whole night, detects snoring and has a smart alarm that wakes you in light sleep, with no subscription, no in-app purchase and a privacy label that says Data Not Collected. Sleep Cycle's help center puts snore detection under Premium. AutoSleep declares Data Not Collected too. It costs $8.99 once and needs an Apple Watch. The other five iPhone apps sell Premium through in-app purchases.

Key takeaways

  • Sleep Cycle has 4.7 stars from 30K ratings on the US App Store, and its website puts the Smart Alarm on the free plan.
  • Sleep Cycle's label, as declared on the App Store on 27 September 2026, puts Identifiers under Data Used to Track You and seven categories under Data Linked to You.
  • Only two apps on this list carry a Data Not Collected label, Snore Timeline and AutoSleep, and AutoSleep costs $8.99 and requires an Apple Watch.
  • Pillow, ShutEye, BetterSleep, AutoSleep and Sleep as Android each describe a smart alarm that wakes you in lighter sleep, and Snore Timeline's Smart Wake does it inside a 15, 30 or 45 minute window. SnoreLab and Snollo have none.
  • Sleep Cycle's help center lists snore detection, sound detection and sleep notes as Premium features. In Snore Timeline, snore detection, full-night audio, export and sleep factors cost nothing.
  • SnoreLab is all about snoring. It has a Snore Score, remedy testing and 57K ratings, but no smart alarm, and Premium runs $5.99 to $59.99.

    What Sleep Cycle does well

    Sleep Cycle has earned its place. The App Store listing shows an Editors' Choice badge and 4.7 stars from 30K ratings, and the developer reports more than 40 million downloads. Most people know it for the Smart Alarm, which the listing says wakes you in your lightest sleep phase, and its Smart Alarm page says you pick a window from 10 to 30 minutes. That page also states that "The Smart Alarm is available on our Free plan."

    You don't need a wearable. It tracks sleep through sound or the phone's accelerometer, detects snoring, coughing and sleep talking according to the listing, and ships a sleep sounds library of stories, soundscapes and guided meditations. For couples, Who's Snoring? uses machine learning to tell your snores from a partner's in a shared bedroom, something its site says sets it apart from other snore apps. If you want an alarm clock first and a sleep tracker second, Sleep Cycle does the job.

    Its 50 most recent US App Store reviews (26 August to 25 September 2026) praise the sleep data (9) and the alarm (7) more than anything else. One reviewer on 23 September 2026 put it as “No more stopping the alarm and falling back asleep.” The subscription is the sore spot, with 17 complaints about it, the trial or cancelling, and 7 of those say they couldn't cancel or got charged anyway. Tracking accuracy comes up 8 times.

    Reasons to look elsewhere

    Price

    The download is free. In-app purchases on the listing run from $2.99 to $57.99, and most carry the Premium name, including a $39.99 item that unlocks all features. The help center's list of Premium features includes snore detection, sound detection, Who's Snoring?, sleep notes, heart rate tracking, online backup and full statistics [15].

    Privacy label

    Sleep Cycle's label lists Identifiers under Data Used to Track You. Apple counts it as tracking when an app links its data with other companies' data for targeted ads or ad measurement, or shares it with a data broker. Under Data Linked to You the label lists seven categories (Health & Fitness, Purchases, Location, Contact Info, Identifiers, Usage Data and Diagnostics), all of it the developer's own declaration. Sleep Cycle's privacy policy says health data stays on your device by default. Turn on backup and everything in the app goes to its servers on Google Cloud, where it stays until you withdraw consent or for up to a year after your subscription ends. With your consent, it uses pseudonymised health data to improve the app, keeps it for up to 3 years, and can share aggregated or de-identified data with partners and in public reports [20].

    Snore detection and Premium

    Sleep Cycle's help center lists snore detection and sound detection as Premium features, along with Who's Snoring? [15]. The free plan keeps the Smart Alarm, sleep analysis and the nightly sleep graph. Recordings you keep can't be exported all at once, the help center says, so you share them one by one [16]. In Snore Timeline, snore detection, full-night audio and export are free. Snore Timeline vs Sleep Cycle compares the two apps.

    1. Snore Timeline

    My app records the whole night through your iPhone mic, AirPods or another Bluetooth mic and puts every snore, gasp, cough and sleep-talking event on a timeline you can zoom. Snores group into episodes, each with its duration, count, peak and average dB and a plain-English summary. Tap any moment to hear it. Breathing pauses it picks up from the audio get flagged as something to show a doctor, and How detection works lays out the rules. It estimates sleep stages (Awake, Light, Deep, REM) from the audio alone and draws them in a band under the snoring chart, which switches between loudness and snores per minute, so you can see whether your snoring clusters in light, deep or REM sleep.

    Smart Wake rings in light sleep inside a window you choose. More on that below. When you snore, Snore Nudge can play a sound, tap your wrist or both, one step louder each time a nudge doesn't help, and exports land in the Files app as CSV plus audio. Every night gets a 0 to 100 sleep score that counts snoring and breathing disruptions among its factors. You can tag nights with any of 23 built-in sleep factors, such as Alcohol or Nasal Strips, or with your own, and after 5 tracked nights, at least 2 tagged and 2 untagged, the app compares them and ranks what helped. On the wrist, the bundled Apple Watch app shows live heart rate and HRV while you record, taps you for Snore Nudge and a silent alarm, and can stop a recording, though starting one happens on the iPhone. Apple Health brings in data from Apple Watch, Oura, Whoop, Garmin and Ultrahuman for the sleep score, recovery score and hypnogram, and when a night has stages from several sources, each keeps its own timeline so you can compare them.

    It's free, with no subscription and no in-app purchases. The label declares Data Not Collected. Its rating is 4.9 from 214 ratings, a small count next to Sleep Cycle's 30K. Snore Timeline reached the App Store in September 2025, Sleep Cycle in 2009, and I build it on my own with no outside funding and no marketing budget.

    Snore Timeline had 38 US App Store reviews in the feed (14 November 2025 to 19 September 2026), and having no subscription or paywall gets the most praise (23), followed by ease of use (11). The one 3-star review is about a partner’s CPAP hose setting off false snore signals that the reviewer couldn't delete. An update days later added the option to delete episodes.

    Pick it if you want a smart alarm like Sleep Cycle's with free snore detection, full-night audio you can replay and export, and a nudge when you snore, all with no subscription and a Data Not Collected label.

    2. Pillow

    Pillow is the Apple Watch pick. According to its App Store listing, it analyzes sleep cycles with an Apple Watch, iPhone or iPad, and its smart alarm aims to wake you at the lightest possible sleep stage. You get heart rate analysis and a stage diagram, and on-device machine learning records snoring and sleep talking, but Smart Wake-up, sound recordings, heart rate analysis and CSV export all sit on the Premium list. Its own guide says Automatic Mode, where you just wear the watch, can't ring an alarm or detect snoring [14]. Snore Timeline's Smart Alarm and recordings are free. Pillow holds an Editors' Choice badge.

    The subscription costs $19.99 monthly, $49.99 annual or $59.99 quarterly. Its label declares no Data Used to Track You, and Data Linked to You lists Identifiers, Usage Data and Diagnostics. Rated 4.4 from 96K.

    Of its 50 most recent US App Store reviews (20 July to 25 September 2026), 17 complain that the app opens on a subscription trial with no way into a free version. That's the top complaint. The top praise, from 12, is that it helps them understand their sleep patterns, and seven doubt its sleep tracking accuracy.

    Choose Pillow if you want the Apple Watch to track sleep on its own, with no iPhone needed, which is how its site describes it. There's more in Snore Timeline vs Pillow.

    3. ShutEye

    ShutEye leads with sound. The App Store listing describes a library of sleep sounds and ambient noise, bedtime stories, custom sound mixes and a sleep tracker that tracks snoring, along with a smart alarm clock meant to wake you gently. According to its FAQ, that alarm wakes you during your lightest sleep phase, and there's no Apple Watch app; ShutEye reads watch data through Apple Health. It has 350K ratings. That's one of the largest counts here.

    It's a subscription. The listing says $59.99 a year, and in-app purchases range from $9.99 to $69.99. On the label, Identifiers appear under both Data Used to Track You and Data Linked to You, and Health & Fitness is declared as not linked. Its privacy policy says health data is never shared with advertising partners, and that sleep data goes to IKEA if you opt in to a joint program for product recommendations [21]. It holds a rating of 4.8 from 350K.

    Trial charges and the paywall top the complaints in its 50 most recent US App Store reviews (30 June to 24 September 2026), with 19, while 11 reviewers say the tracking marked them asleep when they were awake. Praise is scattered. Five reviews say it helped their sleep. No single feature gets five mentions.

    It fits if falling asleep to sounds matters as much to you as knowing when you snored. Snore Timeline vs ShutEye has the details.

    4. BetterSleep

    BetterSleep started life as Relax Melodies. Its App Store listing counts more than 300 sleep sounds and music tracks, more than 250 meditations and more than 100 sleep stories. There's a sleep recorder that picks out when you snore, move around, talk in your sleep or make other common night sounds, plus bedtime reminders and timers. The help center describes a smart alarm that wakes you in lighter sleep inside a window you set, the same idea as Snore Timeline's Smart Wake. Its 391K ratings top the App Store side of this list.

    Subscription again, with in-app purchases from $11.99 to $99.99. The label puts Purchases, Identifiers, Usage Data and Other Data under Data Used to Track You, and seven categories under Data Linked to You. It's rated 4.7 from 391K.

    Price dominates its recent reviews. Thirty-three of the latest 50 on the US App Store (26 August to 26 September 2026) complain about it, and 22 of those say sounds they used for free now need a subscription. On the positive side, 8 say it helps them fall and stay asleep and 5 like the custom sound mixes.

    A good match if a big library of sounds, stories and meditations comes first for you, you plan to subscribe, and a snore check is secondary.

    5. SnoreLab

    SnoreLab does one job. The App Store listing describes snore detection, a Snore Score for intensity, full-night recording (Premium, per its FAQ), BreathFlow breathing analysis, and remedy and lifestyle tracking so you can see what changes your snoring, and you can share recordings by email. It says outright that SnoreLab is not a medical device. SnoreLab has no smart alarm. Its alarm clock ends the session at the time you set [11], and its FAQ sends people who want a smart alarm to its sister app, Sleepwave [12]. There's no snore alarm either [18], and no Apple Watch app. Among the dedicated snore apps here, its 57K ratings are the most.

    Premium comes through in-app purchases from $5.99 to $59.99, a mix of subscriptions and a $7.99 one-time item, and the label lists Usage Data under Data Used to Track You and Health & Fitness, Contact Info, User Content and Identifiers under Data Linked to You. Its privacy policy, updated 18 September 2026, says it collects sleep data such as respiratory rate, breathing disturbances and sleep stages whenever you track, and uses it to show your stats, to tailor content if you consent, and in aggregate to improve the app. Apple Health data reaches SnoreLab only if you opt in to research [19]. It sits at a rating of 4.7 from 57K.

    Reviewers like it most for helping them find what cuts their snoring (10 of the 50 most recent US App Store reviews, 24 April to 26 September 2026). Another group (9) uses it to monitor sleep apnea they already know about, or their CPAP. On the downside, the price, trial or paywall (9) and nights that failed to record or crashed (8) come up most.

    Go for SnoreLab if you want a Snore Score you can set against other users (its FAQ describes this) and a remedy library to read. Snore Timeline's answer is a 0 to 100 sleep score with snoring as one factor, plus 23 built-in sleep factors that it ranks by what helped. Snore Timeline vs SnoreLab compares the two.

    6. Snollo

    Snollo pairs snore recording with Apple Watch data. Its App Store listing says it reads heart rate, respiratory rate and blood oxygen from the watch, shows sleep stages and a sleep score, and runs its acoustic analysis on the iPhone. Without a watch, its FAQ says, stages come from the iPhone's microphone and motion sensors, with the phone on the bed. Recordings and health logs are kept in your private iCloud container, per the listing. It comes with rain, nature and white-noise sounds. Snollo has no smart alarm, per its own comparison with Sleep Cycle [17]. It's also new. With 14 ratings, that 2.9 average rests on few voices.

    A subscription runs $13.99 weekly, $5.99 monthly or $39.99 a year. The label declares nothing under Data Used to Track You, but Data Linked to You covers Health & Fitness, Purchases, Location, Contact Info, User Content and Identifiers. Its privacy policy says sleep data and audio clips live in your private iCloud, and that its analytics tools receive your User ID and app events but no sleep data, audio or health information [22]. The rating stands at 2.9 from 14.

    I could only read 9 US App Store reviews for it (11 March to 17 September 2026), because that's all the feed returned. Five say the app recorded nothing or sat stuck on analysis. No praise theme reaches five.

    Consider it if you want a sleep sounds library and logs that sync through iCloud across your Apple devices, and you don't mind a young app.

    7. AutoSleep

    AutoSleep, from Tantsissa, is Watch-first. Its App Store listing says it tracks sleep from your Apple Watch with nothing to press, and it needs watchOS 4 or later. You get sleep rings, heart rate, blood oxygen, respiration rate, wrist temperature and a smart alarm built into the watch app that aims to wake you in lighter sleep. Snoring intensity comes from a separate app, AutoSnore. The listing cites Best of App Store selections from 2018 to 2023.

    Pay $8.99 up front, and per the listing there are no extra in-app purchases and no subscriptions. The label declares Data Not Collected. Its 62K ratings average 4.7.

    In the latest 50 US App Store reviews (7 to 25 September 2026), 15 credit it with insight that helped them change their sleep habits and 7 praise its accuracy, and the most common complaint, from 9, is gaps or errors in tracking since a recent update.

    It makes sense if you want sleep rings and a smart alarm that run on the watch alone, as the listing describes. For snore data you'd add AutoSnore, a separate $4.99 purchase. Snore Timeline estimates stages from the phone's audio, so it works with or without a watch, and its Watch app can wake you with wrist taps.

    8. Sleep as Android (Android only)

    On an Android phone, Sleep as Android from Petr Nálevka (Urbandroid) covers both the alarm and the snoring. Its Google Play listing describes a smart alarm clock that tracks your sleep cycles and aims to wake you gently at the optimal time. It also lists AI sound recognition for anti-snoring, sleep talk and sickness detection, Sonar contactless tracking, and support for Wear OS, Garmin, Fitbit and other wearables. The listing shows an Editors' Choice badge and 10M+ downloads.

    Pricing starts with 7 days of premium, after which you stay on freemium or upgrade, and the listing notes ads and in-app purchases. Under Data safety, Google Play shows it may share Location, Health and fitness and 2 other types, and may collect Location, Health and fitness and 3 others. It has a rating of 4.6 from 392K reviews.

    There's no App Store listing for Sleep as Android, so I don't have a review summary for it.

    Pick it if you're on Android and want a Wear OS, Garmin or Fitbit watch in the loop, since Snore Timeline's Android app has no watch support. It's a Google Play app. More options are in the best snoring apps for Android.

    Keeping the smart alarm

    Maybe the alarm is what you'd miss most. Seven apps here describe an alarm that picks a lighter moment to wake you: Snore Timeline, Pillow, ShutEye, BetterSleep, AutoSleep, Sleep as Android and Sleep Cycle itself. Pillow keeps Smart Wake-up behind Premium. AutoSleep runs its alarm on the Apple Watch. SnoreLab and Snollo have no smart alarm [11] [17].

    How Snore Timeline's Smart Wake works

    In Settings, under Wake Up, you set a wake-up time and an alarm sound, then pick a Smart Wake window of 15, 30 or 45 minutes. Inside that window the app watches its live sleep staging along with your own movement and sounds, and if you stir or murmur, the alarm can go off right away, since you're already surfacing. A light-sleep reading by itself fires only in the later part of the window. If no good moment comes, it rings at your set time. The sound fades in over the first few seconds. One action stops both the alarm and the night's recording.

    Smart Wake needs Sleep Insights turned on. If you wear an Apple Watch, you can swap the sound for wrist taps and wake up without disturbing a partner, and in case the recording stops overnight, the app also schedules a backup system alarm. Each setting is on the Smart Alarm support page, and Apple Watch & Health covers the wrist side.

    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. Bring recordings to a doctor if you suspect breathing pauses.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is there a free alternative to Sleep Cycle?

    Snore Timeline is free. There's no subscription and no in-app purchase, and it has both a smart alarm and snore detection. On Android, Sleep as Android gives you 7 days of premium and then drops to a freemium tier with ads.

    Pillow, ShutEye, BetterSleep, SnoreLab and Snollo cost nothing to download, but they sell Premium through in-app purchases.

    Which Sleep Cycle alternative has no subscription?

    Snore Timeline, which has no subscription and no purchases of any kind. AutoSleep is $8.99 up front with no in-app purchases or subscriptions, according to its listing, though it requires an Apple Watch. Every other app on this list sells a subscription.

    Does Sleep Cycle detect snoring for free?

    No. Sleep Cycle's help center lists snore detection, sound detection and Who's Snoring? (the feature that separates your snores from a partner's) as Premium features.

    The free plan includes the Smart Alarm, sleep analysis and the nightly sleep graph. Snore Timeline detects snoring for free, with no subscription and no in-app purchases.

    Which alternative collects the least data?

    Snore Timeline and AutoSleep. They're the two apps on this list that declared Data Not Collected on the App Store as of 27 September 2026. Snore Timeline is also free with no purchases; AutoSleep costs $8.99 and needs an Apple Watch.

    Developers fill in these labels themselves. The App Store notes that Apple hasn't verified them, and my privacy label comparison lines up 14 snore and sleep apps side by side.

    Do these apps work without an Apple Watch?

    All but one do. AutoSleep requires an Apple Watch. Snore Timeline, Sleep Cycle, SnoreLab, ShutEye and BetterSleep record with your phone's microphone, Pillow's listing says it works with an Apple Watch, iPhone or iPad, and Snollo's is built around Apple Watch data.

    Snore Timeline estimates sleep stages from audio alone. Wear a watch and it adds heart rate, blood oxygen and other readings.
