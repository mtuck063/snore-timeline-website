Sleep Tracking on iPhone Without a Watch: What Works (2026)

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 8 Nov 2026

Quick answer

An iPhone can’t track sleep by itself. Apple’s sleep tracking needs an Apple Watch. Without one, a sleep app can listen to your breathing, use silent sonar, or feel you move through the mattress. In the one lab study that tested all three, the microphone app came closest to the lab, though the study’s authors work for the company that makes it. I make Snore Timeline, which tracks your sleep stages from breathing sounds and records the whole night for free.

Key takeaways

  • Apple Health: without a watch, the iPhone only sets a sleep goal and schedule. It records no sleep [1].
  • Four methods: phone apps track sleep through the mic, silent sonar, motion through the mattress, or the last time you used your phone.
  • No pulse: heart rate, HRV, blood oxygen and temperature need a watch or ring.

    Can an iPhone track sleep without a watch?

    Not with Apple’s own tools. Apple says you must turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch to receive sleep data; the iPhone’s part is a sleep goal and schedule [1]. A third-party app does the measuring, and many write the night to Apple Health. Snore Timeline writes time asleep, stages and respiratory rate there.

    How a phone senses sleep

    A phone has a microphone, a speaker and a motion sensor. The method an app uses decides where the phone goes and what fools it.

    Snore Timeline uses the mic. After about 15 minutes it learns your breathing baseline, then reads steady breathing as Deep and irregular breathing as REM. Breathing too soft to hear gets a Silence label, never a guess (how sleep stages work).

    What a phone can and can’t measure

    The phone hears the room. The watch feels your wrist.

    How to set up a phone-only night

    Most lost nights come from placement, a dead battery or a forgotten start.

    1. Place the phone for its method. For a mic app, the nightstand 1 to 2 feet from your head. For sonar, level with your chest with the microphone facing you [9]. For a motion app, face down on the mattress 15 to 25 cm from your body [10].
    2. Plug it in. On a low battery iOS may close the app mid-night. Snore Timeline uses about 3 to 5% an hour, and less face down (placement checklist).
    3. Turn on Sleep Focus. It keeps calls from interrupting the recording.
    4. Automate the start. A Shortcuts time-of-day automation can run Snore Timeline’s Start Recording at 11:00 PM every night (Shortcuts setup).
    5. Quiet the room. A fan, air purifier or TV can bury the breathing a mic app listens for.
    6. Sleep alone if you can. SleepScore can’t track two people in one room [6]. A mic hears everyone, so put the phone on your side of the bed.
    7. Keep the setup the same. Same spot, same distance, full nights of six hours or more.

    SleepScore

    SleepScore senses breathing and movement with ResMed sonar and, per its help center, records nothing [6].

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the iPhone Health app track sleep without an Apple Watch?

    No. Apple says sleep data needs Track Sleep with Apple Watch, so the Health app shows sleep only when a watch or another app writes it there.

    Can a phone app track sleep without pressing start?

    Rise can, because it guesses from phone use, though it can count time awake as sleep. Mic and sonar apps need a start, which a Shortcuts automation can handle for you.

    Is a phone app as accurate as an Apple Watch?

    It depends on the method. A microphone hears your breathing, a mattress app feels your movement, and a watch reads your heart rate and movement from the wrist. Snore Timeline uses your watch’s stages whenever one syncs.
