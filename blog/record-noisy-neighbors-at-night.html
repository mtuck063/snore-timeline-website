How to Record Noise From an Upstairs Neighbor at Night

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 9 Oct 2026

Quick answer

Leave a phone recording all night with an app that lists each loud sound with its time and peak decibel reading, then read the list in the morning instead of scrubbing eight hours of audio. I make Snore Timeline, which does this through its Loud Sound Detection setting, with nothing to buy and the audio kept on your phone. Write the times into a noise diary, because landlords and building managers ask for one first.

Key takeaways

  • Record all night: noise upstairs comes at random hours, so a clip you start by hand usually misses it.
  • Read the list: Snore Timeline lists every loud sound with its time and peak dB, so you don’t scrub the audio.
  • Accuracy: phone readings are relative. NIOSH found built-in mics 14 dB low to 11 dB high [3].
  • Diary: a written log of dates and times is what landlords and building managers ask for first.

    How do I record the whole night?

    1. Turn on Loud Sound Detection in Detection Settings. It’s off by default.
    2. Leave the threshold on Automatic. It sits just above your room’s background noise.
    3. Pick the Basic quality tier. It keeps the low frequencies where thuds and bass live, at about 8 MB an hour.
    4. Turn off fans and white noise, plug the phone in and point the mic at the ceiling or shared wall.

    How do I find the noise in the morning?

    1. Open the episode list. Every loud sound shows its start time, duration and peak dB.
    2. Tap one to play it. Playback starts two seconds early, so you hear the room, then the thump.
    3. Remove the misfires, like the cat knocking something over, so the list reflects the neighbor.

    To share, tap the download icon beside an episode, or use Export Report for a spreadsheet of every sound and the full night’s audio. The export page has the steps.

    What can a phone recording prove?

    It proves when. Two weeks of timestamps show a pattern, which is what a landlord or building manager looks for. The decibel number is rougher: NIOSH found built-in phone mics read anywhere from 14 dB low to 11 dB high, and an external mic got within 1 dB [3] [4].

    Read the number as a comparison. A 68 dB thump in a 32 dB room says more than 68 on its own. The WHO puts waking at about 42 dB inside the bedroom [2].

    Keep the recordings between you and whoever handles the complaint. Footsteps aren’t a conversation, but if voices are the noise, check your local recording rules first [1].

    A note on what this app is

    Snore Timeline is a snore recorder that analyzes audio on your phone. It is not a sound-level meter, not a medical device, and not a legal evidence tool. Its decibel readings are uncalibrated estimates for your own reference. It does not diagnose sleep apnea or any condition. If broken sleep from noise is affecting your health, talk to a doctor as well as your landlord.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I use a snoring app to record my upstairs neighbor?

    Yes, if it records the whole night and lists loud sounds as events. In Snore Timeline, turn on Loud Sound Detection.

    Will a phone recording count as evidence?

    It can support a complaint alongside a written diary. Present it as a dated record of when the noise happened, since a phone mic isn’t a calibrated meter.

    How much storage and battery does a night take?

    About 64 MB for eight hours on the Basic tier, and 5 to 7 percent of battery an hour. Plug the phone in.
