How to Record Noise From an Upstairs Neighbor at Night

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 18 Oct 2026

Quick answer

Leave a phone recording all night with an app that lists each loud sound with its time and peak decibel reading. In the morning, read the list instead of scrubbing through eight hours of audio. I make Snore Timeline, which does this through its Loud Sound Detection setting, with nothing to buy and the audio kept on your phone. Write the times into a noise diary, because landlords and building managers ask for one first.

Key takeaways

  • Record all night: noise from upstairs comes at random hours, so a clip you start by hand usually misses it.
  • Read the list: Snore Timeline lists every loud sound with its time and peak dB, so you don’t have to scrub through the audio.
  • Accuracy: treat phone readings as relative. In NIOSH tests, built-in phone mics read anywhere from 14 dB too low to 11 dB too high [3].
  • Diary: a written log of dates and times is what landlords and building managers ask for first.

    How do I record the whole night?

    1. Turn on Loud Sound Detection in Detection Settings. It’s off by default.
    2. Set a Fixed threshold. Automatic sits just above your room’s background noise, so it also logs you turning over. Switch Threshold Mode to Fixed and set the slider 15 to 20 dB above the background level Settings shows. Thuds and bass still clear it.
    3. Pick the Basic quality tier. It keeps the low frequencies where thuds and bass live, at about 8 MB an hour.
    4. Turn off fans and white noise. Plug the phone in and point its mic at the ceiling or the shared wall.

    How do I find the noise in the morning?

    1. Open the episode list. Every loud sound shows its start time, duration and peak dB.
    2. Tap one to play it. Playback starts two seconds early, so you hear the room, then the thump.
    3. Remove the false alarms, like the cat knocking something over, so the list shows only your neighbor’s noise.

    To share one sound, tap the download icon beside its episode. To share the whole night, use Export Report, which saves a spreadsheet of every sound plus the full night’s audio. The export page has the steps.

    What can a phone recording prove?

    It proves when the noise happened. Two weeks of time stamps show a pattern, and a pattern is what a landlord or building manager looks for. The decibel number is rougher. In NIOSH tests, built-in phone mics read anywhere from 14 dB too low to 11 dB too high. With an external mic, readings came within 1 dB [3] [4].

    Read the number as a comparison. “A 68 dB thump in a 32 dB room” tells a landlord more than “68 dB” alone. For scale, the WHO links noise peaks of about 42 dB inside the bedroom to waking up in the night [2].

    A note on what this app is

    Snore Timeline is a snore recorder that analyzes audio on your phone. It is not a sound-level meter, not a medical device, and not a legal evidence tool. Its decibel readings are uncalibrated estimates for your own reference. It does not diagnose sleep apnea or any condition. If broken sleep from noise is affecting your health, talk to a doctor as well as your landlord.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I use a snoring app to record my upstairs neighbor?

    Yes, if it records the whole night and lists loud sounds as separate events. In Snore Timeline, turn on Loud Sound Detection first.

    Will a phone recording count as evidence?

    It can support a complaint alongside a written diary. Present it as a dated record of when the noise happened, since a phone mic isn’t a calibrated meter.

    How much storage and battery does a night take?

    Eight hours on the Basic tier takes about 64 MB. Recording uses about 5 to 7 percent of the battery per hour, so plug the phone in.
