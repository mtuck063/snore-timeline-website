Sleep Talking Recorder App: How to Catch What You Say at Night

Set your phone up to catch speech, whispers and mumbling, then hear every clip in the morning.

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 3 Oct 2026

Quick answer

A sleep talking recorder app listens through your phone's microphone all night, picks speech out from snoring and room noise, and saves each clip so you can play it back in the morning. Snore Timeline does this on iPhone and Android. No subscription, no in-app purchases. It labels clear speech as Sleep Talking episodes. Turn on Loud Sound Detection as well and it also saves the whispers and mumbling the classifier can't name. Tap Transcribe on a clip and the app writes out what you said, on the iPhone itself, at no cost. It keeps the whole night's audio on your phone, and declares Data Not Collected on the App Store. The same recording gives you estimated sleep stages, with no wearable needed. For clear words, pick the Standard or High Fidelity quality tier. Put the phone 1 to 2 feet from your head.

    What sleep talking is

    Sleep talking has a clinical name, somniloquy. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) classes it as a parasomnia, one of a group of unwanted behaviors that happen during sleep. You talk out loud with no awareness of it. Sometimes the words form full sentences, and sometimes they come out as mumbling no listener could follow. It can get loud enough to wake a bed partner.

    It's common. The AASM puts lifetime prevalence at 60% to 65% and reports it in half of young children and in about 5% of adults. The Sleep Foundation cites figures of up to 66% for people who've had an episode, with 17% reporting one in the past three months. Both sources say it affects men and women at the same rate. It seems to run in families, too. It can happen in any stage of sleep.

    When to see a doctor

    For most people, sleep talking is harmless. The AASM says it's benign by itself but may be a symptom of another sleep disorder. Talk to your medical provider, it advises, if episodes increase in frequency, begin at an older age, or come with limb movements or daytime sleepiness. The Sleep Foundation gives the same advice for ongoing sleep talking with other sleep problems or daytime drowsiness.

    Age of onset matters. In a 2015 case report in the Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, sleep physicians note that when sleep talking starts late in life, REM sleep behavior disorder presenting as sleep talking should be considered. The same report shows why a recording helps. A 67-year-old man's partner told him he talked about former girlfriends every night. His own overnight recordings captured no sleep talking at all, and that evidence reshaped the case.

    How a phone app catches it

    A sleep talking recorder keeps the microphone open all night, sorts each sound as it comes in, and saves the stretches you'll want to hear.

    Listening and classifying

    To a classifier, speech and snoring sound different. Snoring carries most of its energy between about 50 Hz and 3 kHz, while speech reaches higher, and consonants like "s" sit in the 1,500 to 8,000 Hz band. Snore Timeline classifies audio the moment it happens, on the phone, with no periodic sampling. It listens for snoring, gasps, coughs, sleep talking, laughter and loud sounds, and it follows your breathing for pauses. The sleep talking classifier needs speech clear enough to recognize. Whispers and mumbling often aren't, and Loud Sound Detection picks those up instead. How Detection Works explains the classifier.

    Saving the clips

    Snore Timeline records the whole night and groups detected sounds into episodes. Speech shows up as Sleep Talking episodes. If talking and snoring overlap, the episode's marked Mixed. Each episode card lists its duration, its peak and average volume in decibels, and an event breakdown that counts sleep talking next to snores, gasps and coughs. Episodes & Events covers every field.

    Playing it back

    On the timeline, sleep talking is cream, snoring is dusty rose and breathing disruptions are pale purple. In the episode list, look for the Sleep Talking badge. When you tap an episode, playback starts about 2 seconds before the speech so you catch the lead-in. For recordings made in a quiet room, the app boosts the low and mid frequencies where speech sits and evens out the volume. A soft murmur comes up to a level you can follow. Timeline & Playback shows the controls.

    Reading what you said

    Playback tells you that you talked. Transcription tells you what you said. On iPhone, open a Sleep Talking episode and tap Transcribe, and Snore Timeline writes the words under each clip. A speech model runs on the phone and does the work, so the audio stays on the device, and it works in airplane mode once the model is downloaded. The first time, the app asks before a one-time download of 574 MB on most recent iPhones.

    Sleep talk is often mumbled, and speech models have a habit of inventing sentences for unclear audio. Snore Timeline marks a clip with no clear words as No recognizable speech and labels a shaky result as a Best guess, so you know when to trust your ears instead. It transcribes in the language you use the app in, and it's free. None of the other sixteen apps in this guide mention transcription in their App Store listings. Sleep Talk Transcription covers the details.

    Setting up Snore Timeline for sleep talking

    Detection works out of the box. Whether you can make out the words the next morning comes down to four settings.

    1. Pick Standard or High Fidelity

    Snore Timeline has three recording quality tiers. They change the saved audio. Detection works the same at all three.

    Snore Timeline recording quality tiers, from the Storage & Quality support page.
    TierFrequency rangeSize per hourSpeech playback
    Basic (Snore-Focused)Favors below 4,000 HzAbout 8 MBCan sound muffled
    Standard (Clear Audio)Up to 8,000 HzAbout 15 MBFull range of everyday speech
    High Fidelity (Full Detail)Up to 24,000 HzAbout 25 MBEvery detail, largest files

    Transcription needs Standard or High Fidelity too, since Basic compresses speech too much for the model to read. Standard covers speech at about 120 MB for an 8-hour night, but if you want every breath and consonant and have room for about 200 MB a night, go with High Fidelity. Storage & Quality explains the Storage Limit and Episode-Only Storage, which drops the silence between episodes and keeps the speech.

    2. Move the phone closer

    For snoring, 3 to 4 feet works. Speech is quieter and more detailed, so set the phone 1 to 2 feet from your head on a nightstand, with the microphone uncovered. Plug it in and lay it face-down, since a full night of recording uses about 5% to 7% of the battery per hour.

    3. Turn on Loud Sound Detection

    Sleep talking detection works out of the box, but it only names speech it can recognize. A lot of sleep talking is whispered, mumbled or half-formed, and the classifier can't call those sounds speech with any confidence, so they never become Sleep Talking episodes. Loud Sound Detection fills that gap. It opens an episode whenever a sound rises above a threshold, whatever that sound turns out to be.

    It's off by default. Open Settings and switch on Loud Sound Detection, and leave it on next to sleep talking detection every night you record. The two together catch clear speech by name and everything quieter or murkier by volume. In the default Automatic mode the app measures your room's background noise every minute and keeps the threshold just above it. Quieter room, lower threshold, more whispers caught.

    4. Quiet the room

    Fans, white noise machines, HVAC and a TV left on cover up quiet sounds, and once a room sits above about 45 dB at rest, more sounds land in the Loud Sound category. If you can't quiet the room, move the phone closer and raise the detection sensitivity.

    Recording through AirPods

    Snore Timeline also records through AirPods and other Bluetooth mics. In Settings, the Microphone option set to Automatic switches to your headphones' mic when they connect, and iPhone Microphone keeps the built-in mic recording while your AirPods play music at full quality. If the input changes overnight, the app keeps recording on the new one. Earbuds hear you from inside your ears, and their battery can run out before morning, so for the steadiest results the troubleshooting page suggests the phone's own mic 1 to 2 feet away. SnoreLab's FAQ tells you to disconnect Bluetooth audio devices before recording [11].

    In the morning

    Open last night, check the event breakdown for a sleep talking count, and play each Sleep Talking episode from the list. On iPhone, tap Transcribe on an episode to read the words while you listen. Then play the Loud Sound episodes. Some will be you turning over or noise from the room, and some will be whispered or mumbled talk the classifier couldn't name. Bright apricot swells in the real-time view are sounds the app counted without naming, and a whisper can hide among them too. Your first week walks through a full review.

    The nightly summary also shows your sleep stages, estimated from the same audio with no wearable or Apple Health needed. On the snoring chart, a band of stages runs underneath, and tapping a moment on the hypnogram marks the same time on the chart. Sleep Stages explains the estimates.

    BetterSleep

    BetterSleep has a library of more than 300 sleep sounds and music tracks. According to the listing, its sleep recorder captures the sounds you make and picks out when you snore, move around, talk in your sleep or make other common noises at night. Its help center says each recording plays as a 5 to 7 second clip, clips are deleted after 20 days, and they can't be downloaded to share [13].

    Pillow

    Pillow tracks sleep stages with an Apple Watch or the iPhone, reads heart rate from the watch and has a smart alarm. Its listing says the machine-learning audio recordings pick up snoring, sleep apnea, sleep talking and other sounds during the night, and that the processing happens on the device. The same listing puts sound recordings, CSV export and Smart Wake-up under Premium features, and its help center says it skips sounds that are too short or seem insignificant [14]. Snore Timeline's versions come at no cost: stages estimated from the audio alone, a Smart Alarm that wakes you in light sleep within a 15, 30 or 45 minute window, and an Apple Watch app with live heart rate and HRV.

    Snore Recorder - Sleep Monitor

    This app says it uses AI to detect and record "snoring, talking, and shouting sounds", and a morning log shows how much you snored, talked or screamed. It lists no in-app purchases on the App Store.

    Sleep Talk Recorder

    Sleep Talk Recorder is the one most people have heard of. Its listing says it records what you say while you're asleep, and that it's not an ongoing recording: a filter qualifies each sound and triggers the recorder.     Privacy matters more for speech

    A snore says little about you. What you say at night is another matter. A recording of it can hold names, worries and things you'd never say awake.

    Apple asks developers to declare what data they collect. By Apple's definition, collecting means sending data off the device and keeping it longer than a request needs, and voice and sound recordings count as Audio Data. On those same labels, SnoreLab lists Audio Data as linked to you, and Snore Recorder - Sleep Monitor lists it as not linked to you. A label entry doesn't by itself tell you which recordings leave the phone. BetterSleep and Pillow don't list Audio Data. Among the ten apps from the App Store search, ShutEye, Remly, Sleep Tracker: Recorder, Sound and Sleep Monitor list Audio Data as not linked to you, Noxogy lists User Content as linked to you, and SnoreClock declares Data Not Collected. Sleep Talk Recorder, Dream Talk Recorder, Sleep Cycle and Sleep Well don't list Audio Data. Of the apps I added on 3 October, Sleep Talking Recorder declares Data Not Collected, and SleepWatch doesn't list Audio Data.

    Snore Timeline's label reads Data Not Collected. The app has no account, no server and no analytics SDK. Classification runs on the phone, and recordings stay in the app until you export one. Transcription follows the same rule. The speech model runs on the iPhone, so the words you said at 3 a.m. never reach a server, mine included. Of the 14 snoring apps I checked, four declare Data Not Collected, and Snore Timeline is the one of those four with no purchases of any kind.     Sharing a clip

    Snore Timeline saves single episodes. You can play one for a partner or a doctor without exporting the whole night.

    1. Open the night and the episode list.
    2. Tap the download icon next to a Sleep Talking episode. It saves the clip with 2 seconds of padding before and after.
    3. Once the icon turns into a share icon, tap it. The iOS Share Sheet opens, and you can send the clip by Messages, email or AirDrop.

    Clips land in the Files app under On My iPhone > Snore Timeline > Snoring Episodes. On Android, look in Downloads. For a doctor, the Export Report sheet bundles CSV files with a Total Sleep Talking Events column for each night, plus episode clips or the full-night recording if you want them. None of it needs a purchase. By contrast, SnoreLab's FAQ lists export as Premium [11], and BetterSleep's help center says its recordings can't be downloaded [13]. Export & Sharing covers both paths. If you'd rather record someone else, Recording a partner covers placement.

    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. Bring recordings to a doctor if you suspect breathing pauses.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is there a free app that records sleep talking?

    Yes. Snore Timeline records all night, labels speech as Sleep Talking and lets you play every clip back. There's no subscription and no in-app purchases, and every feature's included. Snore Recorder - Sleep Monitor also lists no in-app purchases on the App Store, and its listing says it detects talking. BetterSleep, SnoreLab and Pillow are free to download and sell premium plans. Pillow lists sound recordings as a premium feature. Of the ten sleep recorders and trackers I found in my first App Store search, none is free with everything included. Sleep Talk Recorder sells an unlock for all nights, Dream Talk Recorder's free version stops after 2 hours, SnoreClock costs $9.99 up front, and Sleep Cycle, ShutEye, Noxogy, Remly, Sleep Tracker: Recorder, Sound, Sleep Monitor and Sleep Well sell premium plans.

    Can an app transcribe my sleep talking?

    Yes. Snore Timeline transcribes sleep talking on iPhone. Open a Sleep Talking episode, tap Transcribe, and the words appear under each clip. A speech model on the phone does the transcribing, so your recordings stay on the device, and it's free with no subscription.

    Clips with no clear words come back as No recognizable speech instead of invented sentences. Record in Standard or High Fidelity, since Basic compresses speech too far to transcribe. None of the other sixteen apps in this guide mention transcription in their App Store listings.

    Why is my sleep talking recording muffled?

    Usually it's one of two things, the quality tier or the distance. Snore Timeline's Basic tier favors frequencies below 4,000 Hz to keep files small, and that flattens speech. Standard keeps everything up to 8,000 Hz, the full range of everyday speech, and High Fidelity keeps up to 24,000 Hz. Switch in Settings before bed.

    Distance matters as much. Sound fades fast across a room. A phone on a dresser picks up a murmur, where a phone 1 to 2 feet from your head picks up words. Keep the microphone uncovered and the room quiet.

    Is sleep talking a sign of a sleep disorder?

    Most of the time, no. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine calls sleep talking benign by itself and notes that it may be a symptom of another sleep disorder. It advises talking to your medical provider if episodes increase in frequency, start at an older age, or come with limb movements or daytime sleepiness. The Sleep Foundation gives similar advice for ongoing sleep talking alongside other sleep problems or excessive drowsiness during the day.

    A recording can help that conversation, since it shows a doctor when the talking happens and what it sounds like. It does not diagnose anything.

    Can my partner's app record me?

    Yes. Any phone microphone in the room picks up sound from both sides of the bed, so your partner's phone can record your sleep talking. Snore Timeline doesn't separate two sleepers or tag a voice with a name. Put the phone 1 to 2 feet from the head of the person you want to record. If there's any doubt about who's talking, play the clip back.

    Do sleep talking apps upload recordings?

    The App Store privacy label is the public record of what each developer declares. Apple defines collecting as sending data off the device and keeping it longer than a request needs, and it counts voice and sound recordings as Audio Data. As declared between 27 September and 3 October 2026, SnoreLab lists Audio Data as linked to you, and Snore Recorder - Sleep Monitor, ShutEye, Remly, Sleep Tracker: Recorder, Sound and Sleep Monitor list it as not linked to you. BetterSleep, Pillow, Sleep Talk Recorder, Dream Talk Recorder, Sleep Cycle, Sleep Well and SleepWatch don't list Audio Data. Snore Timeline, SnoreClock and Sleep Talking Recorder declare Data Not Collected.

    A label is a developer's own declaration. It tells you what the developer says it collects. It doesn't show where a given file goes.
