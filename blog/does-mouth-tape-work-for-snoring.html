Does Mouth Tape Work for Snoring? How to Test It on Yourself

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 14 Nov 2026

Quick answer

Sometimes. Mouth tape can cut snoring if you snore with your mouth open and breathe easily through your nose. In two small studies of people like that, snoring fell by about half and by about three quarters. It won’t help if the sound comes from your nose. Skip it with a blocked nose, suspected sleep apnea, asthma, COPD, heart disease or nighttime reflux. I make Snore Timeline. To find out if tape works for you, record 3 nights without it, then 3 nights with it, and compare.

Key takeaways

  • Who it helps: people who snore with their mouth open and breathe easily through the nose. In two small studies, snoring fell by about half and about three quarters.
  • Who should skip it: anyone with a blocked nose, possible sleep apnea, lung or heart disease, or reflux.
  • Still snoring: tape only keeps your mouth closed. If you snore through your nose or your tongue falls back into your throat, it won’t help.
  • Self-test: record 3 nights without tape and 3 with it. Snore Timeline then compares them for you.
  • Stop: if you have more breathing disruptions on taped nights, you sleep worse, or the tape irritates your lips or skin.

    Does mouth tape work for snoring?

    For some people. Tape closes the mouth, and that helps when open-mouth breathing drives the snoring and the nose can carry the whole night. In sleeping adults, breathing through the mouth more than doubled airway resistance and raised breathing pauses from 1.5 to 43 an hour in a small crossover study [8].

    What did the studies find?

    Studies of mouth tape or mouth closure in sleep, from the papers themselves. Most compared one night without tape with one night with it [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7].
    StudyWho took partWhat they found

    Huang 201530 mouth breathers, mild apneaSnoring fell by about three quarters and got quieter

    Lee enrolled people who could tolerate the tape; Huang excluded allergies, a crooked septum and facial hair. Ioerger’s bundle added nasal strips, a nasal rinse and side sleeping, and partners rated the result, so tape’s own share can’t be separated out.

    It works against you in three cases:

    • A blocked nose. Nasal obstruction added about 14 breathing pauses an hour in a 2026 meta-analysis [9]. People with stuffy noses breathe through the mouth because they need to.
    • A collapsing soft palate. For heavy mouth breathers with this pattern, the mouth is the bypass, and closing it cut airflow [4].
    • Moderate or severe apnea. No study shows tape treats it. Lee’s authors say it isn’t recommended there [2].

    Is mouth taping safe? Who should not tape

    No formal AASM position statement exists. An AASM spokesperson told National Geographic the evidence is scant and that people with lung conditions or known apnea should be cautious [15].

    Who should not tape, from the Sleep Foundation [13], Cleveland Clinic [14] and the Rhee review [1].
    WhoWhy
    Blocked noseCold, allergies, sinusitis, deviated septum: no air route left
    Suspected apneaLoud snoring with gasps, daytime sleepiness: see a doctor first
    Asthma or COPDTape only if a doctor advises it

    Anxiety or panicCan trigger panic on waking

    I’d add heavy drinking nights and sedatives to that list. Alcohol worsens apnea and raises the risk of vomiting. That’s my reasoning; no medical body says it.

    Why am I still snoring with mouth tape on?

    Snoring comes from vibrating tissue in the throat, mostly the soft palate (the soft back part of the roof of your mouth), and it shifts with position, sleep stage and the route you breathe through [10]. Tape moves one of those. Common reasons it didn’t help:

    • You snore through your nose. In Hsia’s group, 68% snored during nasal breathing [7].
    • Air leaks past the tape. Some sleepers puff air out through taped lips. In Jau’s study, the people whose oxygen didn’t improve puffed more [5].
    • Your tongue falls back. Tape doesn’t move the tongue. A jaw-advancing mouthpiece beat the tape bundle in Ioerger’s trial [6].
    • The tape came off. A night where it peeled off at 2 a.m. is a mixed night, and it muddies the comparison.

    How to test mouth tape on yourself

    One night proves nothing. Snoring varies between nights [10], and in 30 people with apnea, breathing pauses per hour changed between two lab nights in about 85% of them, driven by time on the back [11]. Snore Timeline’s Mouth Taping factor turns the trial into a with-and-without comparison.

    1. Screen yourself. Close your mouth and breathe through your nose for a few minutes. Then wear the tape for an hour in the daytime [13]. If either feels hard, stop here.
    2. Record 3 nights without tape. Keep the phone 1 to 2 feet from your head and plugged in. These count as untagged nights.
    3. Tag what else happened. Mark Alcohol, Congestion or Slept on Back on the nights they apply, so they don’t pass for tape effects.
    4. Tape for 3 nights. In the morning, open the night’s summary, tap Tag Sleep Factors, pick Mouth Taping and swipe the picker down to save.
    5. Tag only the nights the tape held. If it came off early, leave the tag off. Skip Repeat nightly, or the app has no untaped nights to compare.
    6. Change nothing else. No new nasal strips or mouthpiece during the test, the same room noise, and the same phone in the same spot.
    7. Go back to no tape for a few nights. A second untaped stretch keeps a cold or a stressful week from landing on one side. The app looks back 8 weeks, so all of it counts.

    The app compares once it has at least 3 taped and 3 untaped nights, and shows Building Insights until then. If the result looks close, add a few more nights of each. The app’s Mouth Taping guide sets no trial length and says to stop if it’s uncomfortable. The remedy testing guide covers the factor screens.

    When to stop

    • Disruptions rise on taped nights. The app reads this as a warning to check your nose. If they keep showing up, take the recordings to a doctor. Breathing disruptions explains how the app flags them.
    • You wake gasping or fighting the tape. Take it off and don’t push through.
    • Your skin or lips get sore. Irritation and allergy are the most common side effects listed [13] [14].
    • Your nose blocks. Skip tape on cold and allergy nights, and leave the tag off.
    • Several nights show no clear effect. Tape isn’t your fix. Test something aimed at a different cause, like side sleeping or a mouthpiece.

    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. Mouth tape is not a treatment for sleep apnea. If you snore loudly with gasps or wake tired, see a doctor before you tape, and bring your recordings.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does mouth tape help sleep apnea?

    Two small studies saw fewer breathing events in mild apnea, and none showed benefit in moderate or severe apnea. Get tested before you tape; the safety list explains why.

    How long does mouth taping take to work?

    The positive studies measured a change on the first taped night or within a week. Try 3 nights with tape and 3 without. If the difference looks small, add a few more nights of each, since snoring changes from night to night.

    Can I use mouth tape with CPAP?

    In a 2025 crossover trial of 62 mouth breathers on CPAP, tape added about 52 minutes of nightly use [12]. That was tape alongside CPAP under a sleep clinic’s care, so ask yours first.

    Should I try nasal strips and mouth tape together?

    Test them one at a time, nose first. If you start both in the same week, a drop in snoring can’t tell you which one earned it. How to test nasal strips covers the strip side.
