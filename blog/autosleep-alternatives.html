AutoSleep Alternatives (2026): vs Apple Health and Snore Timeline

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 8 Nov 2026

Quick answer

I make Snore Timeline, a free AutoSleep alternative with no subscription, no trial and nothing locked. With an Apple Watch, it picks up your nights on its own and builds a recovery score from them. It also records the night on your iPhone, so you get sleep stages on nights the watch charges and snoring you can play back. AutoSleep costs $9.99 once and runs its own analysis of your watch data, with no sleep schedule to set up. Apple’s built-in Health app is free and gives you a sleep score, stages and vitals from the same watch, but no smart alarm.

Key takeaways

  • Price: AutoSleep costs $9.99 once. Apple Health comes free with the iPhone. Snore Timeline is free with nothing to buy.
  • Automatic tracking: all three log your watch nights with nothing to press. Apple Health tracks once you turn on Track Sleep with Apple Watch, and Snore Timeline picks up those nights on its own. AutoSleep runs its own detection with no sleep schedule.
  • No watch: AutoSleep and Apple Health record no sleep from an iPhone alone. Snore Timeline tracks sleep stages from your breathing sounds.
  • Sound: AutoSleep’s snoring data comes from AutoSnore, a separate $4.99 app. Apple Watch doesn’t track snoring.

    The apps, one by one

    Snore Timeline

    Snore Timeline records through the iPhone mic, estimates Awake, Light, Deep and REM from your breathing and replays every snore and sleep-talking moment. With a watch it uses the watch’s stages in the sleep score and adds a recovery score.

    • Free tier: everything, including the Smart Wake Up Alarm, Snore Nudge and the Watch app.
    • Price: None [13].
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected.
    • Reviews: 40 of 41 US App Store reviews (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026) give 5 stars. 22 praise that it’s free with no ads or subscription, 19 talk about snoring, 12 call it easy to use and 6 mention sleep talking.
    • Limits: the phone sits 1 to 2 feet away, plugged in.

    AutoSleep

    AutoSleep tracks nights and naps from the Apple Watch with no button, Sleep Focus or schedule [2]. It adds a sleep bank, readiness and a silent watch alarm [4].

    • Free tier: none. It’s a paid app.
    • Price: One-time, $9.99, no in-app purchases [1]. AutoSnore, for snoring, is a separate one-time $4.99 [7].
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected. Its site says no data upload and no analytics [2].
    • Reviews: 94 of 369 (Jan to Nov 2026) say it taught them about their sleep. Export is CSV [6].

    Apple Health

    Apple Health is built in. The watch estimates REM, Core and Deep sleep and scores the night from duration (50 points), bedtime consistency (30) and interruptions (20) [8].

    • Free tier: all of it, with an Apple Watch [8].
    • Price: None, built in.
    • Privacy label: none; it’s part of iOS.
    • Extras: Vitals flags overnight changes [11], sleep apnea notifications run on supported models [12], and Readiness needs Series 12 or Ultra 4 [10].
    • Needs: 30% charge at bedtime and an hour of wear [8].

    Pillow

    Pillow tracks automatically from the Apple Watch and can use Apple’s stages. Without a watch, the phone lies on the mattress.

    • Free tier: automatic watch tracking and heart rate [15], after you start and cancel a 7-day trial [16].
    • Price: Premium, $19.99 to $59.99. Smart wake-up and audio are Premium [14].
    • Privacy label: Data Linked to You: Identifiers, Usage Data, Diagnostics.
    • Reviews: 57 of 284 sleep reviews (Sep 2025 to Nov 2026) complain about the subscription.

    Bevel

    Bevel reads sleep from an Apple Watch or another wearable and sets it beside recovery, strain, stress and nutrition.

    • Free tier: recovery, sleep, strain, stress, the Watch app and a smart alarm for Apple Watch [18].
    • Price: Pro and credits, $4.99 to $99.99. Pro adds an AI coach [17].
    • Privacy label: Data Used to Track You: Identifiers.
    • Reviews: 45 of 78 sleep reviews (Sep 2025 to Nov 2026) mention recovery; 33 the AI coach.

    Athlytic

    Athlytic builds recovery scores from Apple Watch HRV, resting heart rate and the sleep the watch logs to Apple Health [19].

    • Free tier: its site documents a 7-day trial and nothing free after it [20].
    • Price: Pro, $4.99 to $29.99.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected.
    • Reviews: 48 of 74 sleep reviews (Jan 2025 to Sep 2026) mention recovery; 13 give 1 or 2 stars.

    SleepMatic

    SleepMatic detects falling asleep, waking and naps from the Apple Watch with no session or schedule [21].

    • Free tier: none. It’s a paid app.
    • Price: One-time, $1.99, no in-app purchases.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected.
    • Reviews: not in my sample.

    Sleep++

    Sleep++ reads what the Apple Watch logs and shows stages, heart rate, blood oxygen and a Readiness Score. Version 4.8 dropped its Watch app and needs iOS 26 [22] [23].

    • Free tier: automatic tracking and the last 7 nights [23].
    • Price: One-time, $1.99, unlocks full history.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Linked to You.
    • Reviews: 35 of 50 sleep reviews (Jan 2025 to Nov 2026) give 1 or 2 stars, many over the new history limit.

    Which one to pick

    • Pick Snore Timeline if you want it free: watch nights logged on their own, sleep stages from the phone on nights the watch charges, a sleep score, a recovery score, snoring you can replay and a smart alarm.
    • Pick AutoSleep if you want its own analysis of your watch data with no sleep schedule to set up, for $9.99 once.
    • Pick Apple Health if you want nothing to install.
    • Pick Bevel if you want strain, stress and nutrition in the same app as sleep.
    • Pick Athlytic if you train and want recovery scores built around your workouts.
    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. Bring recordings to a doctor if you suspect breathing pauses.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is AutoSleep better than Apple’s built-in Health app?

    It does more with the same watch, naps and a smart alarm included, with no schedule to keep. Apple’s app is free, and AutoSleep can show its stages, so many owners run both.

    Does AutoSleep work without an Apple Watch?

    No. Its listing says it requires one, and even its time-in-bed mode uses the watch. For phone-only trackers, see the best free sleep tracker apps for iPhone.

    Can I use Snore Timeline and AutoSleep together?

    Yes. Both read and write Apple Health, which charts one sleep source at a time, so choose the leader under Sleep, then Data Sources & Access. AutoSleep tracks the watch while Snore Timeline adds the audio.

    Why did AutoSleep stop tracking my nights?

    After iOS 27, a new Health permission screen tripped up many owners. AutoSleep’s FAQ gives the fix: reinstall, restart both devices and allow every permission.
