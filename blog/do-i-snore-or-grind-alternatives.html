Do I Snore or Grind Alternatives (2026)

By Menelik Tucker · Updated 9 Oct 2026

Quick answer

Do I Snore or Grind is a free SleepScore Labs app that records snoring and teeth grinding, lets you listen the next morning, and compares nights with and without a remedy. Its last iPhone update shipped in March 2019, and of its 25 most recent App Store reviews, 16 say it records nothing on current iPhones and 7 say sign-up fails. I make Snore Timeline, which records the whole night with nothing to buy and no account, labels snoring and sleep talking, and saves grinding as episodes you judge by ear.

Key takeaways

  • Updates: Version 1.2.4 carries a 2019 date on both stores, and reviews since January 2025 average under two stars, several naming iOS 18 [1] [2].
  • Account and data: Sign-up is required, the privacy policy never names the app, and Play says it may share audio with third parties [2] [3].
  • Remedies: 15 of 137 reviews praise its remedy comparison, more than any other feature; Snore Timeline’s 23 sleep factors do that job for snoring.
  • Grinding vs clenching: A microphone hears grinding and misses clenching, and a 2026 review says only audio plus video tells the two apart [9].
  • Diagnosis: No app diagnoses bruxism. Dentists work from history and an exam, and the AASM calls a sleep study a rare need for grinding alone [6] [10].
  • Snore Timeline: No grinding label. Loud Sound Detection saves anything above the room’s background as an episode, grinding included, with full-night audio.

    What a phone can hear: grinding makes a sound, clenching doesn’t

    Most grinding episodes last up to a second, skip some nights, and cluster in stages one and two of non-REM sleep [6] [7]. The 2018 international consensus defines sleep bruxism as masticatory (chewing) muscle activity, rhythmic or not; in otherwise healthy people it’s a behavior and no disorder, though it can signal one in people with sleep apnea or REM behavior disorder [8]. Muscle is the operative word: a jaw can clench in silence, and that form does much of the damage.

    What can each method detect?

    What catches grinding, catches clenching, and can diagnose bruxism, per the AASM [6], Sleep Foundation [7], the 2026 review [9], NIDCR [10] and Cleveland Clinic [11]. Partly = catches signs without separating the two, or gives a less definitive result.
    MethodCatches grindingCatches clenchingDiagnoses
    Phone micYesNoNo
    Bed partnerYesNoNo
    Dentist examPartlyPartlyYes
    Home sleep testNoPartlyPartly
    Sleep lab, EMG + videoYesYesYes

    Every app on this page listens through the phone mic, which hears the scrape in a quiet room. Treat every grinding count here, my app’s episodes included, as a cue to listen and then book the dentist.

    • Phone mic: Bruxa’s listing says a phone across a bedroom can’t do it “reliably enough to put a number on” [16].
    • Bed partner: You may not know you grind unless a bed partner or family member hears it, per the AASM.
    • Dentist exam: The dentist asks what your partner noticed and checks for worn teeth and pain or tenderness in the jaw and face. Per Cleveland Clinic, that settles most cases [10] [11].
    • Home sleep test: Without audio or video (ambulatory PSG), it can’t hear grinding or see jaw movement and logs muscle activity without separating clenching from grinding, so the Sleep Foundation calls it less definitive [7] [9].
    • Sleep lab: The 2026 review names jaw-muscle EMG with synchronized audio and video most accurate, and the AASM calls a sleep study a rare need for grinding alone [6] [9].
    • Sleep apnea: Its link with grinding is real and its direction unclear, per Cleveland Clinic [11].

    The alternatives

    Bruxa, GrindMute and SnoreLess had fewer than five ratings each when I checked, and Sleep Island 23. Nobody has tested them for you yet. Only SnoreLab’s listing names a remedy comparison [15] [16] [17] [18] [19].

    SnoreLab

    SnoreLab records snoring only, shows other sounds as gray noise bars, and claims nothing about grinding [14]. Its Snore Score and remedies tracking match what Do I Snore or Grind users liked, the same job Snore Timeline’s sleep score and sleep factors do.

    • Free tier: Samples, with playback limited per night [14].
    • Price: Premium, $5.99 to $59.99, for full-night recording, unlimited playback and export [13].
    • Privacy label: Data Used to Track You, for Usage Data.
    • Reviews: 4.7 (57K), the largest user base here; updated 5 October 2026 [13].

    SleepyNight

    SleepyNight’s listing names audio detection of snoring, sleep talking and teeth grinding, plus sleep stages, decibel trends and a sleep score [15]. It’s the one maintained app over 200 ratings with a grinding label, so try it if that label matters more to you than price.

    • Free tier: Free to install; the listing doesn’t say whether it keeps clips or the whole night.
    • Price: Subscriptions, $6.99 to $29.99, including a weekly plan.
    • Privacy label: Data Used to Track You, for Usage Data.
    • Reviews: 4.7 (208); last updated 2 July 2025.

    Sleep Island

    Sleep Island saves clips of the night’s sounds and lists possible teeth grinding among them [19].

    • Free tier: Free to install, with clips.
    • Price: Premium, from $2.99 a month.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Linked to You.
    • Reviews: 4.9 (23), too few to sample.

    Bruxa

    Bruxa records the whole night on the device, ranks the night’s moments, and has you mark each as grinding or not; its listing says it won’t count grinding [16].

    • Free tier: The seven most recent nights.
    • Price: One-time, $14.99, for PRO.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected.
    • Reviews: 3.0 (2), too few to sample.

    SnoreLess

    SnoreLess labels teeth grinding alongside snoring, sleep talking and coughs, from short highlights [18].

    • Free tier: Everything, with no account and no ads.
    • Price: None.
    • Privacy label: Data Not Collected.
    • Reviews: 1.0 (1), too few to sample.

    GrindMute

    GrindMute saves a clip when it hears a grinding-like pattern, can play a gentle sound to interrupt it, and lets you mark each clip. That sound is the closest thing here to the old Anti-Snore Wearable feedback [17].

    • Free tier: Free to install; some features need Pro.
    • Price: Premium, from $6.99 a month.
    • Privacy label: Data Linked to You [17].
    • Reviews: No ratings yet.

    Medical note

    Snore Timeline analyzes audio on your phone. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose bruxism, sleep apnea or any other condition. A Loud Sound episode is a sound to listen to, not a grinding count. Dentists diagnose bruxism from your history and an exam, and a sleep lab is the only place grinding is measured with muscle sensors and video. Worn teeth, jaw pain or morning headaches are reasons to book the appointment whatever an app recorded.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the Do I Snore or Grind app still work?

    It’s still listed on both stores with a 2019 build, and the review history shows what broke and when. Two of the 25 most recent reviews are positive, so it may still run on an older phone with an existing account.

    Is Do I Snore or Grind free?

    On the App Store it’s free with no in-app purchases listed, and it needs an account. The Google Play description says you’ll be asked to use a paid version after five nights, though no purchase shows on either store.

    Which app detects teeth grinding?

    Do I Snore or Grind and SleepyNight claim a grinding label with more than 200 ratings; the four specialists under 25 ratings each sit in the table. Snore Timeline has no grinding label and saves grinding as episodes you confirm by ear.

    Can a phone app diagnose bruxism?

    No. A microphone catches the sound of grinding and misses clenching, which is silent, and the methods table shows what each test catches. Dentists diagnose bruxism from your history and an exam, and the lab standard is jaw-muscle EMG with synchronized audio and video.

    How does Snore Timeline handle teeth grinding?

    It doesn’t label it. Turn on Loud Sound Detection in Detection Settings, record on Standard or High Fidelity for the clearest scrape, and the Snore Timeline section covers the threshold, playback and remedy comparison.
