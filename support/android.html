Snore Timeline for Android

The same night, on your Android phone.

By Menelik Tucker · Published 13 Jun 2026 · Updated 7 Nov 2026

On this page

  • Wearables through Health Connect
  • How it differs from iPhone

Snore Timeline runs on Android phones with Android 10 or later. It records your sleep sounds, labels them on an interactive timeline, and analyzes your night the same way the iPhone version does. It now matches the iPhone app on nearly every feature, from wearable import and sleep talk transcription to Snore Nudge and the smart wake window. This page covers what you get on Android, how Health Connect brings in your watch or ring, the few things that stay iPhone-only, where your files end up, and the battery settings that keep an overnight recording alive.

What the Android app includes

The Android app shares the core of the iPhone version. While you sleep, a sound classifier on the phone listens to the microphone and labels what it hears. You get:

  • Sound detection. The app recognizes snoring, gasps, coughs, sneezes, sleep talking, and breathing, along with louder distress sounds. Each detection lands on the timeline. How Detection Works covers the labels.
  • An interactive timeline. Your night is drawn as a waveform you can zoom from a real-time view of the live recording out to a 1-hour overview, with decibel readings in approximate dB SPL. A calendar lets you jump to any recorded night. See Timeline & Playback.
  • Episodes and events. Snores close together get grouped into episodes, and a 30-second gap starts a new one. Each card carries gasp and cough badges, a waveform strip you can tap to play, and a snores-per-minute chart on episodes longer than 5 minutes. Episodes & Events explains the grouping.
  • Breathing disruption detection. After a minute of audible breathing, the app watches for silent stretches of 10 seconds or more, then confirms with a recovery sound. Disruptions are marked on the timeline. It's audio analysis, and it isn't a medical test. See Breathing Disruptions.
  • Sleep stages and a hypnogram. The app estimates awake, light, deep, and REM stages from your breathing and movement sounds and charts them across the night. See Sleep Stages.
  • A sleep score, Sleep Insights, and weekly trends. Each night gets a score out of 100 with the factors behind it, a Sleep Insights card with your hypnogram and breathing rate, and a weekly summary that compares nights and points out what changed. Tag sleep factors, like a mouth guard or a late drink, and the weekly view shows what helped. See Sleep Score.
  • Watch and ring data through Health Connect. Turn it on and the app imports sleep stages and overnight vitals from your wearable's app, shows them in a Sleep Vitals card, and adds a Recovery score. The next section covers it.
  • Sleep talk transcription. Tap Transcribe on a sleep-talking clip and a speech model on the phone writes out the words. The model is a one-time download of about 265 MB or 547 MB, depending on your phone's memory, and it needs a 64-bit phone. See Sleep Talk Transcription.
  • Snore Nudge. When snoring is confirmed, the phone plays a short tone, vibrates, or sends a tap to a paired smartwatch, and the morning chart marks each nudge with a verdict on whether the snoring stopped. See Snore Nudge.
  • A sleep schedule. A bedtime reminder, a recording delay of up to 180 minutes in 5-minute steps, and a wake-up alarm that ends the recording. The alarm can play a built-in tone or a sound you record or import, and a smart wake window of 15, 30, or 45 minutes wakes you in light sleep. See Smart Wake Up Alarm.
  • A Tonight page and tips. Before you record, the app checks what could cut the night short, like a low battery or missing microphone access, and shows a recap of recent nights and a tip for tonight.
  • Audio playback and exports. Play back any moment, export a full night or individual episode clips, and build a report with summaries, sleep phases, events, and audio across a date range. See Export & Sharing.
  • Recording quality, storage controls, and a sleep goal. Choose from three quality tiers, set an optional storage limit so old nights clear out on their own, and set a sleep goal from 6 to 10 hours that drives the sleep bank and the goal bar in the recording notification. See Storage & Quality.

Everything runs on your phone. No accounts, no cloud uploads. The only download the app ever makes is the optional speech model for transcription, and that download sends nothing back. The Privacy Policy has the details.

The Android app comes in 12 languages: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Dutch, Portuguese, Turkish, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese.

Tip

New to the app? The Getting Started guide walks through your first recording. Nearly all of it applies to Android. Where it mentions Sleep Focus, use Do Not Disturb or Bedtime mode, and the Android battery prompt is covered below.

Wearables through Health Connect

Health Connect is Android's shared health store, the counterpart of Apple Health. Snore Timeline both writes to it and reads from it, so a watch or ring you already wear can sit beside your snoring in the morning. It's off by default.

Turning it on. Open Settings and, under Integrations, switch on Google Health Connect. Android asks you to grant the write and read permissions together, and the app then imports your wearable's history, up to 2 years of nights, and shows how many it found. The Tonight page offers the same switch as Sync your watch or wearable. If Health Connect isn't installed on your phone, the switch takes you to its Play Store page.

What it writes. After each recording: your sleep session and stages, and your breathing rate during sleep. Any app you allow can read them, such as Google Fit, Samsung Health, Garmin Connect, Fitbit, Oura, or Withings.

What it reads. Sleep stages, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, blood oxygen, breathing rate, and skin temperature, from any app that writes them to Health Connect: Oura, Whoop, Garmin Connect, Fitbit, Google Fit, Samsung Health, Polar Flow, Withings, Zepp, Ultrahuman, COROS, Suunto, Mi Fitness, and others. Snore Timeline only reads what those apps have written and never writes on their behalf. Where Health Connect offers it, the app also asks to read in the background, so new nights arrive on their own about once an hour.

What you see. In Sleep Insights, a picker lets you switch between the app's own analysis and each wearable's. Pick a wearable and you get its hypnogram, a Sleep Vitals card with each vital rated against your own 28-night baseline (it says Building until you have 7 nights), and a Recovery score. Nights your wearable recorded while you didn't run the app still appear, marked on the calendar, and they count in your weekly summary. When a wearable reports deep and REM stages, your sleep score uses them in place of the audio estimate, and the Recovery score feeds into it too, so a night's score can shift once the wearable's data lands.

  • A basic tracker that reports sleep without stages shows the night as Asleep, with no deep or REM split, and the score leaves its stages factor out.
  • HRV on Health Connect is measured as RMSSD, so the app rates it only against your own baseline, never against a population range.
  • Skin temperature shows as Wrist temp, the label the iPhone app uses, even when a ring measured it.
  • Older nights missing? Tap Re-import under Google Health Connect in Settings. It pulls every source again without overwriting anything.

Sleep Score explains how wearable stages and Recovery change the score, and Apple Watch & Wearables describes the same features on iPhone.

How it differs from iPhone

A few features are platform-specific. This is what changes when you move from iPhone to Android.

Not on Android:

  • No Apple Watch app. The watch app, the live wrist view during a recording, and the silent wrist-tap alarm are iPhone-only. On Android, your wearable's sleep and vitals come in through Health Connect instead. See Apple Watch & Wearables for the iPhone side.
  • No Siri or Apple Shortcuts. Voice control and the Shortcuts automations described in Siri, Shortcuts & Widgets don't exist on Android.
  • No Apple Health. Android uses Health Connect, covered above.
  • No home-screen widgets. The recording notification and the Tonight page carry the same information.

Android equivalents:

  • App shortcuts. Long-press the app icon for quick actions: start a recording, stop one, jump to last night, or open your weekly summary.
  • Do Not Disturb or Bedtime mode in place of Sleep Focus. The Tonight page reminds you to turn it on before you record.
  • A Live Update in place of the Live Activity. On Android 16 the recording notification also shows as a chip in the status bar. Earlier versions get a notification with the same contents.
  • Send Feedback in Settings opens the contact form in your browser with your app version and phone model filled in, the same as on iPhone.

Where your files are saved

When you export audio or a report, the files go to your phone's Downloads folder, sorted by date:

  • Downloads > Exported Recordings holds full-night audio files, each named with the date and length.
  • Downloads > Snoring Episodes holds individual episode clips.
  • Downloads > SnoreTimeline Reports holds Export Report bundles: the CSV summaries, sleep phases, and events for a date range, plus audio if you included it.
  • Downloads > SnoreTimeline holds the CSV files from a single night's download button.

To find them:

  1. Open your phone's Files app.
  2. Go to Downloads.
  3. Open the folder you want, then the dated folder inside.

Since the files are in Downloads, any app that can read your downloads can open or share them. The Export Report sheet bundles summaries, sleep phases, events, and optional audio for a date range and lets you share the result straight from the app. Export & Sharing covers the full report.

Tip

Pick the audio mode that fits what you're after. Full-night files for context, episode clips when you only want the snoring. Episode clips are far smaller, so they're easier to share over email or chat.

Battery and background setup

Recording runs for hours with the screen off, so Android's power management matters more here than it does for most apps. A few things keep an overnight session alive.

Let the app run in the background. The first time you tap Record, right after the microphone permission, Android asks whether to let Snore Timeline always run in the background. Tap Allow. That exemption from battery optimization is what stops Android from putting the app to sleep mid-recording. If you said no, the Tonight page shows a Keep recording all night row whose Allow button brings the prompt back, and your phone's app settings have the same switch under Battery.

Keep the recording notification visible. While the app records, a notification sits on your lock screen. It reads "Settling in" until you fall asleep, then shows your time asleep with a bar that fills toward your sleep goal, plus the night's episode counts. On Android 16 it also appears as a chip in the status bar. That notification is what lets Android keep the microphone running in the background. Don't dismiss it or block the app's notifications, or the recording can get cut short.

Pick a microphone. By default the app records through the phone's own microphone, even when Bluetooth headphones are connected, so music keeps playing at full quality. To record through a headset's mic instead, choose Headset microphone under Microphone in Settings. Other audio on the headset drops to call quality while recording.

  • Plug the phone in. Overnight recording works on battery, but charging takes away any chance of Android closing the app to save power.
  • Put the phone face-down on a nightstand 30 to 60 cm from your head. That keeps your breathing audible, which disruption detection and sleep stages both depend on.
  • Watch out for aggressive power management. Some manufacturers add their own app-killing layer on top of Android. If recordings keep stopping overnight, look in your phone's settings for a per-app battery or background option and set Snore Timeline to unrestricted.

The app also looks after the session by itself. If a phone call or another app takes the microphone, the notification changes to "Recording paused" and the recording resumes on its own once the microphone is free; after 30 seconds it also sounds an alert so you know. At 10% battery while unplugged, it stops recording to save power. In the morning, a banner on the night's summary lists any gaps or an early stop, so a short night never goes unexplained.
