Snore Nudge on Apple Watch

A tap on the wrist when you snore, so you change position without waking up.

On this page

  • Turning it on
  • How the app paces nudges
  • Reading the results in the morning
  • Good to know

A lot of snoring eases up when you roll off your back. The problem is that you're asleep when it matters. With Snore Nudge, when the app hears you snoring live, your Apple Watch taps your wrist, gently enough to get you to shift position without waking you all the way up. Every nudge is logged with what happened next, so in the morning you can see whether the taps quieted your night.

What Snore Nudge does

While a recording is running, the app listens for snoring the same way it always does. With Snore Nudge on, it also responds. The moment snoring is confirmed, your watch gives you a short burst of three gentle taps. It's the elbow from a partner, without the partner.

The taps come through Apple Watch notification mirroring, so the whole thing works with your iPhone locked on the nightstand and the watch app closed. Wear the watch to bed. That's the entire requirement. If the watch is in silent mode you feel the taps with no sound. With sound on, a soft chime goes with them on the watch, and never on the phone.

The app won't nudge before it thinks you're asleep, won't nudge while your wake-up alarm is ringing, and won't nudge in the last stretch before your alarm time, when waking you would cost more than the snoring does.

Turning it on

Snore Nudge is in Settings under Detection, underneath the snoring toggle it depends on, and it only appears when an Apple Watch is paired to your iPhone. It's off by default. Turning it on asks for notification permission if the app doesn't have it yet, since the taps are delivered as mirrored notifications.

Use Test Nudge to feel the pattern before you give it a night. Tap the button, lock your iPhone, and the test taps arrive on your wrist a few seconds later. Locking the phone is the trick, because iOS only mirrors notifications to the watch while the phone is locked.

Tip

For firmer taps, open the Watch app on your iPhone and set Sounds & Haptics to Prominent. The strength of each tap is set by watchOS, so this one setting is the difference between subtle and impossible to miss.

Waking up cancels everything. Dismissing a nudge notification on the watch, or just unlocking your iPhone, clears any taps still queued up.

How the app paces nudges

A nudge that fired at every snore would wreck the sleep it's meant to protect, so most of the work went into pacing. After each nudge, the app watches what happens next. How long you stay quiet afterward is the measure of success.

  • A nudge that worked resets the night. If snoring comes back, the next nudge can come after a couple of minutes.
  • A nudge that didn't work gets retried quickly at first, then with doubling patience. The gaps stretch from about 2 minutes through 4, 8, 16, 32, and up to an hour while the snoring continues. One miss proves nothing, so the backing off starts on the second miss in a row.
  • A night that won't budge ends the experiment. After 10 nudges in a row with no effect, the app stops for the night instead of tapping someone who clearly isn't responding. There's also an overall cap of 12 nudges per night.

The messages on the watch follow the night along. The first nudge, a retry with a position suggestion, and a final attempt are all worded differently, so if you surface enough to read one, it tells you where things stand.

Reading the results in the morning

Every nudge is recorded with what happened next, and you review them the next morning on the snoring chart at the top of your nightly summary. On a night with nudges, the chart's key gets a row counting them, for example 9 Snore Nudges. Tap that row and a hairline marks each nudge across the night, so you can see whether the taps landed in your loudest stretch or your quietest.

A pager steps through them one at a time, with the nudge and its time, like Nudge 9 of 10 · 4:46 AM. Each one gets a verdict, from Stopped snoring for 2 min or Effective down through Slowed, Brief dip, Sped up, and Snored through. The timeline above follows the pager, so you can play the audio around any nudge and hear the result yourself.

Underneath, the app describes the nudge in plain words. How hard you were snoring going in, how long the quiet lasted, whether the rate fell or kept climbing over the next few minutes, and what the tap cost you in wakefulness. That last part is the one that matters most, and it cuts both ways. "You stayed asleep through it, so the nudge cost you nothing," or "The quiet had a cost: you woke 40s after the nudge and were awake for 6 min."

The app won't claim a tap woke you when all the timestamps show is that you woke after it. It reports what it measured and leaves the conclusion to you.

Over a week or two this adds up to a personal answer on whether position nudging works for you. Some people go quiet for an hour after a single tap. Others sleep straight through 12 of them. Your own nights will tell you which you are.

Good to know

  • Snore detection has to be on, since nudges are a response to live detection. Everything runs on the device as usual, and nudging doesn't add any new data collection.
  • The watch has to be on your wrist. iOS only delivers the taps to a worn watch, and a worn watch stays unlocked on its own with wrist detection on, which is the default. There's nothing to do at bedtime. Just don't leave it on the charger, or no taps will arrive.
  • Check your Sleep Focus. The taps are time-sensitive notifications, which Sleep Focus allows by default, but a custom Focus that blocks them will block the nudges too.
  • Position nudging helps positional snorers most. If your snoring doesn't depend on position, expect the nudge verdicts to say so. Snore Nudge is a comfort feature. It isn't a treatment and makes no medical claims.
