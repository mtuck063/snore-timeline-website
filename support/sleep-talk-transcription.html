Sleep Talk Transcription: Read What You Said

Tap Transcribe on a sleep-talking clip and the words appear under it, written out on your iPhone.

By Menelik Tucker · Published 1 Oct 2026

Snore Timeline already saves every stretch of sleep talking it hears as its own episode, so you can play it back in the morning. Transcription goes one step further. Tap Transcribe on a clip and you can read what you said, without putting on headphones and replaying a mumble five times. A speech model on your iPhone does the work, and your recordings stay on the device.

What transcription does

Each Sleep Talking episode holds one or more clips of speech. When you tap Transcribe, the app feeds those clips to a speech recognition model that runs on your phone and writes the words under each clip. The audio stays where it is, so you can read the text and play the clip side by side.

Nothing gets uploaded. Once the model is on your phone, transcription works in airplane mode, and no server, mine included, ever hears your recordings. Sleep talk can include names and things you'd never say awake, which is why the app keeps it all on the device.

Transcription is part of the free app. There's no subscription and no per-clip charge.

The first time: a one-time download

The speech model is too large to ship inside the app, so the first time you tap Transcribe, the app asks before downloading it. The sheet shows the exact size for your phone. iPhones with 6 GB of memory or more get the larger, more accurate model, 574 MB. iPhones with less memory get a smaller one, about 265 MB.

You download it once and the app reuses it every night after. The download keeps going if you leave the app, and you can follow it or cancel it in Settings > Detection Settings, under Sleep Talking, in the Sleep Talk Transcription row. When it finishes, the app installs the model and transcribes the clips you asked for.

This download is the only time transcription needs a connection.

Transcribing a clip

  1. Open a night and scroll to the episode list. The sort and filter button next to the list can narrow it to sleep talking.
  2. Find a Sleep talking episode. The Transcribe button sits on its card, above the signal count.
  3. Tap Transcribe. If the episode holds several clips, the button reads Transcribe 3 clips (or however many there are) and does them all at once.

You can also transcribe one clip at a time. Tap the chevron next to the signal count to open the clip list, then tap the transcribe button beside a clip. A short clip takes a couple of seconds on a recent iPhone. The first transcription after the download takes a little longer while the app loads the model.

The app saves each transcript with its episode, so it's there the next time you open the night.

Reading the result

Each clip gets one of three results:

  • The words you said, when the model heard them clearly.
  • Best guess, in italics, when it picked out words but isn't confident in them. Play the clip to check.
  • No recognizable speech, when the clip doesn't hold words the model can make out.

A lot of sleep talking is mumbled, whispered or half-formed. The app marks those clips as unrecognizable rather than filling them with invented sentences, which speech models tend to do with unclear audio. The recording stays available to play back either way, and your ear is often better than any model at a 2 a.m. mumble.

Language

The app transcribes in the language you use Snore Timeline in, which is your iPhone's language unless you've set a different one for the app in iOS Settings. The model covers about 99 languages. If yours isn't one of them, the app transcribes in English.

If you talk in your sleep in a different language from the one your phone uses, change Snore Timeline's language in iOS Settings > Snore Timeline > Language before you transcribe.

Good to know

  • Record in Standard or High Fidelity. Basic quality compresses the audio too much for speech, so the app can't transcribe nights recorded at that tier. Switch in Settings to transcribe future nights. Storage & Quality compares the tiers.
  • Transcription waits while you record. The model and the recorder would compete for memory, so the app holds transcription until you stop recording. Tap Transcribe during a recording and the clips run once you stop.
  • The audio has to still be on your phone. If the Storage Limit or a manual cleanup removed a night's audio, its clips can't be transcribed. Episode-Only Storage keeps the episodes, so it doesn't get in the way.
  • Distance matters. Speech is quieter than snoring. Setting the phone 1 to 2 feet from your head gives the model clearer words to work with.
  • It's an iPhone feature. The Android app records and detects sleep talking but doesn't transcribe it yet.

Removing the speech model

The model takes up space on your phone between uses. To free it, go to Settings > Detection Settings and tap Remove in the Sleep Talk Transcription row. Transcripts you've already made stay with their episodes. Tap Transcribe again later and the app offers the download again.
