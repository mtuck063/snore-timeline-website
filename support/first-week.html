Your First Week with Snore Timeline

From your first recording to reading a week of patterns.

Someone told you that you snore. Now you want to know how much, how loud, and whether it's getting worse. One recording starts to answer that, and a week of them answers it properly. This guide goes night by night through your first seven, so you know what to do each evening and what to look at each morning, without reading too much into any single night. Keep in mind the app is only listening to sound. It's for your own awareness and can't diagnose anything.

Night one: get set up and record

Tonight you have one job: get a clean recording. Five minutes before bed is plenty.

  1. Open the app and allow microphone access. iOS asks the first time you record, and the record button stays grayed out until you say yes. That's the only permission recording needs.
  2. Check your storage. You need about 500 MB free to start. If you're low, the app tells you how many megabytes you have and waits for you to clear some.
  3. Put the phone 1 to 2 feet from your head on the nightstand, plugged in and face-down. Close means clear audio. The charger stops iOS from killing the app on a low battery, and face-down turns off the always-on display so the battery lasts.
  4. Quiet the room. Turn off fans, white noise, and any TV left running if you can. Once the room's resting level goes above 45 dB, more sounds get labeled Loud Sound instead of snoring.
  5. Tap the microphone button at the top of the screen. The green circle becomes a red stop button. Lock the phone and go to sleep.

In the morning, tap the same button to stop. That's all there is to it. Getting Started has more on placement and permissions if you want it.

Tip

Try for at least four hours in a session. Shorter recordings work, but a full night gives the app more to go on, and you'll get a clearer read on how your snoring changes.

The first morning: what to look at

When you stop the recording, Snore Timeline builds a nightly summary. Start there before you dig into the timeline. It gives you the quick version of the night:

  • Your sleep score and its band (Excellent, Good, Fair, or Poor). Tap the score badge and it expands into a factor-by-factor breakdown of what helped and what hurt.
  • Your snoring activity, counted in snoring signals. More signals, more snoring.
  • Breathing disruptions and sleep talking, counted next to the snoring.
  • A breakdown of time in Light, Deep, REM, and Awake, plus your average respiratory rate.

Then open the night itself. The main screen draws your sleep as a waveform you can scroll through, and the episode list jumps you to detected episodes so you don't have to scrub the whole night. Want to hear the loudest stretch? Scan the episode list for the highest dB value, tap it, and play. If you'd rather poke around by hand, long-press anywhere on the waveform and it jumps to the nearest episode.

Hearing yourself snore for the first time is a bit of a shock for most people. Note the headline numbers and leave it there for now. One night is one data point, and where you slept, what you ate, or a stuffy nose can swing it a long way. Episodes & Events explains how snores get grouped into episodes (a new one starts after a 30-second gap), and Timeline & Playback covers reading the waveform and playing audio.

Nights two through seven: build the habit

The app pays off over many nights, so the goal this week is to make recording automatic. Remembering is the hard part, and a few things help with that:

  • Bedtime reminder. Turn it on in Settings under Sleep Schedule and pick a time. The default is 10:00 PM. Tap the nightly notification and the app opens ready to record.
  • Recording Delay. If you'd rather not record yourself reading in bed, set a delay of up to 180 minutes, in 5-minute steps. The app counts down and starts listening when the timer runs out. Nothing during the delay gets recorded.
  • Siri and Shortcuts. Start a session by voice, or on a schedule you set up yourself. See Siri, Shortcuts & Widgets for the full list.

Keep two things the same all week, so the nights are comparable:

  1. Same placement. Same spot on the nightstand, same 1-to-2-foot distance, every night. Distance changes how loud everything registers, so if you move the phone your numbers move with it.
  2. Same room. A fan running on Tuesday but not Wednesday changes what the app hears. Keep the background as steady as you can.

If a recording cuts out overnight, turn on Sleep Focus or Do Not Disturb and close other audio apps before bed, then check Troubleshooting. A short or missed night isn't a problem. Skip that one and keep going.

What a full week shows you

Once you have several nights recorded, open the Weekly Summary. This is the screen the week was building toward. It's a 7-day overview of your snoring and sleep, compared against the week before:

  • Week-over-week change on each metric, so you can see whether things went up or down. Most show a percentage. Peak Volume shows the difference in decibels.
  • Daily charts for the last 7 days, so you get the trend night by night instead of one lumped total.
  • Snoring metrics like Snore Time, Snoring Signals, and Signals Per Hour, plus your average Sleep Score and the week's Top 5 Notable Episodes.

The comparison is what makes any single number mean something. Your first week is your baseline, which is to say it tells you what's normal for you. After that, a number deserves attention when it moves away from that baseline. On its own, a high or low number tells you very little. A loud night after a late dinner and three drinks is just a loud night.

Your first week has nothing before it to compare against, so the change reads 0% in a neutral color. That's expected. It turns into a real comparison once week two is in. Sleep Score covers the weekly averages and what feeds the score.

Where to go next

You've got a week of data and a habit. Where you go from here depends on what brought you:

  • You want to show this to a doctor. Export a report or the raw files, and read Preparing Doctor-Ready Data. Export & Sharing covers the formats.
  • You noticed breathing pauses. Breathing Disruptions explains what the app marks, the conditions it needs, and what a flag does and doesn't mean.
  • You want to test whether something helps. Mouth tape, a side-sleeping pillow, skipping the nightcap. Testing a Remedy shows how to compare before and after.
  • You want more sleep data. Getting Deeper Sleep Data and the Apple Watch app add biometrics to your nights.
  • You're curious how it works. How Detection Works explains the sound types and the confidence behind each label.

Everything runs on your device. No account, no cloud. The Privacy Policy has the details, and the support hub lists every topic if you need a starting point.
