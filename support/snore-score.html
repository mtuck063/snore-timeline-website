Snore Score

One number for how much you snored. Higher means less snoring.

By Menelik Tucker · Published 14 Nov 2026

On this page
  • How the score works
  • The three parts
  • Where you'll see it
  • What moves your score
  • Nights with no score

The Snore Score sums up a night of snoring in one number from 0 to 100. Every night starts at 100, and snoring takes points away for how long you snored, how loud it was, and how often the snores came. A night with no snoring keeps all 100, so a higher score means less snoring. It runs the same way as your sleep score, where higher is better too.

How the score works

Three parts each take away up to a set number of points. What's left is your score.

  • Duration takes away up to 40 points.
  • Loudness takes away up to 30 points.
  • Snore Rate takes away up to 30 points.

A night where you snored most of the time, loudly and without a break, scores near 0. A night with a few soft snores scores in the 90s. The score is worked out from the same snoring the app shows on the snoring chart, on your phone, from your own recording.

The three parts

What each part measures, and when it takes away all its points.
PartPointsWhat it measuresAll points gone at
Duration40Share of your sleep spent snoring90% of your sleep
Loudness30Share of your snoring that was loudHalf your snoring
Snore Rate30Time spent in nonstop snoringThree quarters of your sleep

Duration is the plainest one: the more of your sleep you spent snoring, the more points it takes. The row reads something like "69% of your sleep was spent snoring."

Loudness counts your Audible and Heavy snoring, the two loudest levels on the snoring chart. The app measures each snore against the background noise of your room, so a fan or a noisy street doesn't make you sound louder. If you snored for less than a quarter of the night, loudness takes away fewer points, so one loud burst on a quiet night stays a small dent.

Snore Rate looks at nonstop snoring: 10 or more snores a minute for at least 3 minutes in a row, about one snore with every breath. Snoring that comes and goes takes fewer points.

The nightly rows show the points each part took away, like "−17", or 0 when it took none. Tap "How is the Snore Score calculated?" in the app to see the full breakdown for that night, with a per-minute chart of your snore rate.

Where you'll see it

  • Your nightly summary. The Snore Score sits right under the snoring chart, above the sleep score. Tap the header to see the three parts and the breathing disruption card below them.
  • The Weekly Summary. A Snore Score card shows your average for the week, the change from last week, and a bar for each night.
  • Sleep factors. When you log sleep factors, the comparison shows how your Snore Score moved on nights with and without it. It needs at least 3 nights on each side and a difference of 5 points or more. Testing a Snoring Fix walks through it.
  • The Tonight recap before you start recording, when your score is low or moved by 10 points or more.
  • Your export. Nightly Summaries.csv has a Snore Score column. See Export & Sharing.

What moves your score

  • Where your phone sits. A phone on the pillow hears louder snoring than one across the room. Keep it in the same spot to compare nights.
  • A partner's snoring. If your phone can hear it, it counts. Tracking a Partner covers setups for two people.
  • Snore detection sensitivity. A higher setting catches more quiet snoring, which can lower your score. A lower setting can raise it. Pick one setting and leave it while you compare nights. How Detection Works explains the levels.

The Snore Score measures sound, not your health. It doesn't feed your sleep score, which already counts snoring on its own terms.

Nights with no score

A night with no snoring shows "No snoring" and counts as 100 in your weekly average. A night with less than 30 minutes of sleep gets no score.
