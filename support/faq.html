About & privacy

What makes Snore Timeline different from other snore tracking apps?

Snore Timeline keeps the full night's audio and lays it on an interactive timeline you can scrub through and tap to hear. It records continuously instead of sampling, so a quiet hour shows up as a quiet hour. Coughs, gasps, sleep talking, and breathing pauses each get their own category, and sounds that happen close together are grouped into episodes you can play back.

Sleep stage data comes in from Apple Watch, Oura Ring, Whoop, Garmin, and Ultrahuman. Detection runs on your phone. No cloud, no account, no subscription.

Does Snore Timeline require a subscription?

No. Snore Timeline is free, with no subscription. Everything is included: breathing disruption detection, episode grouping, nightly summaries, and data export. There are no in-app purchases.

Is my data private?

Yes. All audio processing happens on your iPhone. There are no cloud uploads, no external servers, and no account. Your recordings stay on your device and nobody else can get at them. If you want to share data with a doctor, you export it yourself.

Recording & setup

Where should I place my phone while recording?

For snoring alone, 3 to 4 feet away works fine. If you want breathing disruption detection, put the phone 1 to 2 feet away, since it has to be able to hear you breathe. A nightstand next to the bed, plugged in, is the usual spot.

Where to put your phone →

Which audio quality setting should I choose?

The quality settings are there to save storage. If space isn't a problem, use High Fidelity. Only drop to Basic (~8 MB/hour) or Standard (~15 MB/hour) if you need the room, since both trim the higher frequencies to shrink the file. If you want to hear yourself breathing in recordings you need High Fidelity (~25 MB/hour). Basic and Standard cut out the subtle frequencies where breathing sits.

Can I use it with AirPods or Bluetooth devices?

Yes. Snore Timeline works with Bluetooth audio devices including AirPods, so you can record through the night without running the phone's battery down.

You also pick which mic records. Choose iPhone Microphone in Settings and the phone records while your AirPods keep playing music at full quality, or Automatic to record through your headphones' mic whenever they're connected.

How much battery does it use?

Recording uses roughly 3 to 5 percent of battery per hour, depending on your iPhone. Over a full 8-hour night that adds up, so keep the phone plugged in while it records.

Yes. Say “Hey Siri, start recording in Snore Timeline” or “Hey Siri, stop recording in Snore Timeline.” It's hands-free, which is handy at bedtime when the phone is already on the nightstand.

Detection & accuracy

How accurate is the snoring detection?

Snore Timeline uses Apple's sound classification to analyze your audio continuously through the night, in real time. There's no calibration or setup, and detection starts the moment you press record. The app identifies snoring, gasps, coughs, breathing, and sleep talking by looking at both the shape of each sound and its frequency makeup. All of the processing happens on your device.

How detection works →

How does the snoring app show episodes?

Snore Timeline groups snores that happen close together. When it hears snoring, it opens an episode. If there's no snoring for 30 seconds, that episode ends, and the next snore starts a new one. Each episode shows its duration, snore count, and a plain-English summary, so you can see when in the night you snored most.

How snores group into episodes →

Does it detect breathing pauses?

Yes. Snore Timeline listens for breathing pauses in your sleep audio. It looks for silent stretches of 10 seconds or more followed by a gasp or some other recovery sound, and marks those breathing disruptions on your timeline so you can see the pattern. This is audio analysis only. The app isn't a medical device, and if you're seeing pauses it's worth bringing them to a healthcare professional.

Why does background noise affect detection?

Snore Timeline classifies sounds by their acoustic profile. In a room where the baseline noise is above about 45 dB (a loud fan, a window AC, traffic), snores can get classified as Loud Sound events instead, because the raised noise floor changes how the sound comes through.

If you record in a noisy room, expect more events under Loud Sound. For the cleanest classification, keep the room quiet and put the phone close enough that your breathing stays audible.

How background noise affects detection →

What dB threshold should I use for Loud Sound Detection?

Loud Sound Detection creates an episode whenever a sound goes over the dB threshold you set. It's useful for catching sounds that are hard to classify, like whispers or mumbling that don't register as sleep talking. Snoring and sleep talking are detected on their own without it. To find the right threshold, start a recording and stay quiet while you watch the live dB meter. Note your room's ambient level (usually 30 to 40 dB), then set the threshold 15 to 20 dB above that so only real sounds trigger episodes.

Loud Sound Detection and thresholds →

Can it detect teeth grinding (bruxism)?

The sound classifier focuses on snoring and sleep talking, so it does not label teeth grinding (bruxism) as its own category. Turn on Loud Sound Detection and the app flags any sound above a threshold you set, which captures the noise of grinding and drops it on your timeline to play back and review. It records the sound and nothing more. If you grind often, mention it to a dentist.

Sleep stages

How does Snore Timeline estimate sleep stages?

Snore Timeline listens to how regular your breathing is through the night, using the phone's microphone. About 15 minutes in, the algorithm has your personal breathing baseline. From there it sorts each period into Awake, Light Sleep, Deep Sleep, or REM using thresholds from published research. Steady, regular breathing suggests deep sleep, and irregular breathing points to REM. The algorithm also knows how a night is shaped, with deep sleep early and REM toward morning. Since the app only has audio to go on, stretches where your breathing is too soft to hear get labeled Silence and credited as restful sleep instead of guessed at. These are estimates from audio, and they aren't clinical measurements.

How sleep stages are estimated →

How accurate are the sleep stage estimates?

The estimates are based on published research into how breathing regularity relates to sleep stages. They give you a fair picture of your general sleep architecture and your trends over time. They haven't been validated against polysomnography (a clinical sleep study), so treat them as estimates. For a clinical sleep analysis, see a healthcare professional.

Research that stages sleep from a single physiological signal has reached roughly 70% agreement with polysomnography.

Sleep stage accuracy →

Does sleep stage detection require any special setup?

No. Stage estimation runs on its own from the same recording as snoring and breathing disruption detection. Put the phone 1 to 2 feet away on the nightstand and start recording. The algorithm needs about 15 minutes to work out your breathing baseline. For the most consistent results, put the phone in the same place each night.

Devices & Apple Health

Does Snore Timeline work with Apple Watch?

Yes. Snore Timeline includes an Apple Watch companion app bundled with the iOS app. From your wrist you can stop an in-progress recording with a tap, see live heart rate and HRV while you record, and check a morning sleep dashboard with your hypnogram and snore activity.

A Sleep Score complication shows last night's score on your watch face, and a Smart Stack widget brings the current recording's Live Activity onto the Watch. The Watch app is iOS only.

The Apple Watch companion →

Can the app nudge me to stop snoring?

Yes, with an Apple Watch. Turn on Snore Nudge in Settings under Detection and the watch taps your wrist when the app hears you snoring, which is usually enough to get you to shift position without fully waking. It works with your iPhone locked and the watch app closed. You wear the watch to bed and that's it.

The app paces itself. Quick retries while the snoring goes on, backing off when the nudges aren't working, a cap of 12 per night, and a full stop after 10 misses in a row. The next morning, the snoring chart in your nightly summary pages through every nudge with its outcome, so you find out whether position nudging quiets your snoring.

Snore Nudge on Apple Watch →

Can the alarm wake me gently, or without waking my partner?

Both. Smart Wake gives the alarm a window of 15, 30, or 45 minutes before your set time and rings when it finds you in light sleep, instead of pulling you out of deep sleep at the exact minute. If no good moment comes up, it rings at your set time as usual, and the alarm always fades in.

With an Apple Watch, Wrist nudge on Apple Watch delivers the alarm as gentle taps, alone for a fully silent wake or alongside the sound. Dismissing on the watch stops the alarm, the taps, and the recording together.

How the smart alarm works →

Does Snore Timeline integrate with Apple Health?

Yes. Snore Timeline reads from and writes to Apple Health.

Writes (all derived from your overnight audio, analyzed on-device):

  • Sleep analysis (time asleep, time in bed) from continuous audio.
  • Sleep stages (REM, deep, light, awake, silence) estimated from breathing regularity.
  • Respiratory rate during sleep, counted from breath sounds.

Reads (measured by your wearable): sleep stages, heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate, blood oxygen (SpO2), and wrist temperature from any HealthKit-compatible device.

When you first turn the integration on, the app imports your full sleep history from Apple Health, up to 2 years of nights, so your history is filled in from the start. Apple Health is encrypted local storage on your device. Nothing gets uploaded to a cloud.

Apple Health integration →

Can I see my heart rate, HRV, and SpO2 alongside my snoring?

Yes. When you wear a HealthKit-compatible wearable overnight, Snore Timeline pulls those metrics into a nightly biometrics card showing your averages for breathing rate, heart rate, HRV, blood oxygen, and wrist temperature.

Each metric gets a rating such as Excellent, Good, Typical, Low, or Elevated, plus an inline sparkline so you can see how it changed across the night. Resting heart rate and HRV earn Excellent when they clearly beat your own baseline. Breathing rate tops out at Typical. The ratings are informational and aren't a medical diagnosis.

Export & sharing

Where are my recordings saved?

You can export the whole night's audio as one file, or download all of your snoring episodes at once. For a fuller export, tap the download icon to open the Export Report sheet, pick a date range, and include summaries, sleep phases, events, biometrics, and audio if you want it. The report comes out as CSV files plus whatever audio you chose to bundle.

Exported files are saved to your device, organized into two folders:

Exported Recordings Full night recordings
Snoring Episodes Individual episode clips

On iPhone: open the Files app, tap Browse, choose On My iPhone, then open the Snore Timeline folder.

On Android: open the Files app, go to Downloads, then open Exported Recordings or Snoring Episodes.

Where your files are saved →

Can I share my recordings with others?

Yes. Recordings are saved to your device and can also be shared anywhere via the share sheet: AirDrop, Messages, email, and more.

To share the full night, navigate to the night, tap the download icon at the top of the Snoring Episodes list, and select Export Full Night Audio. To share an individual episode, tap the download icon on any episode card to save it, then tap it again to open the share sheet.

Exporting and sharing audio →

How do I export a week or a month of data at once?

Open Export Report from the night you want to anchor on and choose a date range: Last 7 days, Last 30 days, or All time. Toggle which categories to include: Nightly Summary, Sleep Stages, Detected Events (snoring, sleep talking, coughs, gasps, breathing disruptions), and Watch Biometrics.

If you want audio, the app only pulls recordings for the night you're currently viewing, whatever date range you chose for the stats. It shows a size estimate before generating, then packages everything as one ZIP file you can share with a doctor, a sleep clinic, or anyone else.

Troubleshooting

Why did the app stop recording before morning?

This usually happens when iOS interrupts the app's audio session while it's running in the background. Anything that plays a sound (a phone call, an alarm from another app, some notifications) can cause an interruption. The app tries to resume on its own, but if the interruption went on too long, iOS won't let it restart until you reopen the app.

Fix: Turn on Do Not Disturb or a Focus mode before you start recording. That blocks the calls and notification sounds that would interrupt the session. You can also turn on the Bedtime Recording Reminder so a one-tap notification starts your recording at the right time each night.

Why recording can stop overnight →

How do I restart the app?

Force-quit Snore Timeline, then reopen it. On iPhone, swipe up from the bottom of the screen and pause in the middle to open the App Switcher. On older iPhones with a Home button, double-tap Home instead. Swipe up on the Snore Timeline card to close it, then tap the icon on your Home Screen to relaunch and start a new recording.
